# StoreQL — UI Guide

> A screen-by-screen, persona-by-persona tour of the interface: what a user sees and can do on every screen, not how it's built. Pair this with [README.md](../README.md) (the product tour these screens serve) and [docs/API-GUIDE.md](API-GUIDE.md) (the API each screen calls). For the underlying app structure (state management, routing internals, networking), see [docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §13](ARCHITECTURE.md#13-the-frontend-storeql-app).

## Persona & role model

A signed-in user's role decides both where they land and what they can reach:

| Role | Persona | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `PLATFORM_ADMIN` | Platform Admin | Global superuser for the SaaS operator; never needs onboarding; confined to the Platform Console |
| `OWNER` | Tenant Admin | Business owner; granted automatically when a new tenant/store is created |
| `MANAGER` | Tenant Admin | Also treated as "admin" for routing purposes |
| `STOREKEEPER` | Warehouse operator | Admin shell restricted to the shop floor's pages — **Inventory**, **Fulfilment**, **Food safety**, **Recalls** and **Stores** (zones); home is Inventory |
| `CASHIER` | POS | Access to the POS only (till open is staff-allowed; close/Z-report stays manager+) |
| `CUSTOMER` | Storefront | Default role on self-registration; also the fallback/default persona |

> **Roles that map to real work:** `STOREKEEPER` can call `/admin/inventory/**` and the supporting store/zone/tenant reads (backend `AdminAuthorizationFilter` staff-admin tier). They cannot manage staff, pricing, catalog, or reports. `CASHIER` can open a till under `/admin/cash/**`; closing the till and cash drops remain manager+.

**Routing rules:**
- Unauthenticated users land on the sign-in screen (a separate one for the Platform Console vs. everyone else).
- The public storefront is reachable by anyone — guest or signed in — and is never hijacked by the login/onboarding/platform redirects.
- Signed-in Owner/Admin users with no business set up yet are routed straight into onboarding until it's complete.
- A Platform Admin is confined to the Platform Console; everyone else is blocked from it. Non-admins are blocked from the Admin Console; pure customers are blocked from the POS.
- Each role has a home screen it lands on right after signing in: incomplete onboarding → the setup wizard; platform admin → Platform Overview; admin → Dashboard; cashier → the POS Sale screen; everyone else → the Storefront.

**Shared navigation chrome:** every console uses the same responsive shell (`AdaptiveNavShell`). The Admin Console's 31 pages sit under six headings in a labelled panel beside the content from 1200px and in a slide-out drawer below it, with *Find a page* (Ctrl+K / ⌘K); the Platform Console (five destinations) has a side rail with an icon/label toggle from 800px and a drawer below it; Storefront (Shop/Cart) and POS (Sale/Tender/Cash/Pending) — both well within Material's 3–5-destination range — have the rail from 800px and a persistent bottom navigation bar below it, per Material 3's compact-width guidance. There's no separate "mobile app" vs. "web app" — it's one responsive experience throughout.

## 1. Auth

| Screen | What the user does |
|---|---|
| **Sign in / Register** | A single card that toggles between "Sign in" and "Create account." Registering asks for email, optional phone, and a new password within iam-svc's **published** policy (`GET /auth/password-policy`: 15 to 128 characters by default, NIST SP 800-63B-4), read before the form shows so the rule is the server's own number, with the platform's defaults when it cannot be read. The rule is said under the field before anything is refused by it — *Use at least 15 characters. A phrase of a few words is easiest to remember and hardest to guess; spaces are fine.* — and each of iam-svc's refusals comes back in its own words: too short or too long (in the server's own number when its message names one, so the card never shows two rules), *The password must not be, or contain, your email address.*, or *This password appears in known data breaches and would be guessed. Choose another.* Signing in holds the password to no length — it only asks that one is entered (*Enter your password*), because a wrong password of any length is simply wrong. **Forgot password?** under the sign-in form opens the request page (never on the platform console's sign-in). On success a new account gets the Customer role and, if it's an Owner-to-be, is routed straight into onboarding. Errors (bad credentials, duplicate email, network failure) show as friendly, translated messages; **Show password** / **Hide password**, *Sign in with your business* and its dialog, and every single-sign-on refusal are translated too. |
| **Forgot your password?** (`/forgot-password`, no sign-in; from the storefront's dialog `?from=storefront`, so *Cancel* and *Sign in* lead back to the shop) | *Enter your email address. If an account uses it, we'll send a link to reset the password.* **Send link** always ends in the same words — *If an account uses that address, we have sent a link. It works for 30 minutes, once.* — whether or not an account uses it (only a failed connection says otherwise). The request carries the language the app is running in, so the email comes in it where the platform has it. One email lists every login that uses the address — the shopper account and each business the person is staff at, named — each with its own link. |
| **Choose a new password** (`/reset-password/:token`, the link in the email; no sign-in) | The password rule first, then *New password* and *Confirm new password*; a short or mismatched password is refused before anything is sent, and the server's own refusals (too short, too long, the email itself, breached) come back in words and leave the link usable. **Change password** ends with *Password changed — sign in with it.* and a way to the sign-in (*Shopping from the storefront? You can sign in from the shop.*); it signs nobody in, and a second step is still asked. A used, expired or replaced link: *This link has expired or was already used — ask for a new one.* with **Ask for a new link**. Every other session of that login is ended. |
| **Platform Console Sign-in** | A deliberately separate sign-in screen for platform operators — a tenant staff credential can't be used here, and vice versa. Email + password only (the password must be entered; no length rule applies at sign-in); no self-registration, and no *Forgot password?* — the platform administrator's password and second factor come from the operator's bootstrap, and an email link must never be a way into the console. |
| **Pay your invoice** (`/pay/:token`, 21.12) | The page a late-payment notice's pay link opens. It has no session and asks for none — the business may already be suspended and unable to sign in — and does one thing: pays the one invoice the link names, then says so as money (*Invoice … is settled — £29.00. If your service was interrupted, it is back: sign in as usual.*). A link for an invoice since paid, or from an older notice than the newest, opens nothing and the page says so. |

**Flow:** a new business owner registers → immediately lands in the onboarding wizard, since they have no business set up yet.

## 2. Onboarding

A two-step wizard, shown full-screen right after registration, with a visible step indicator. Its app bar carries **Sign out**, so a login routed here by mistake — a member of staff who signed up instead of being invited — can leave without creating a business.

**Step 1 — Business details:** business name (required), legal/registered name (optional), country, currency (auto-suggested from the country, editable), and — when the platform has plans on sale — the plan, the default one picked, each with its price in the business's own currency where it has one (when the plans cannot be read the business starts on the standard plan and can change it later). **Continue** (its arrow an icon that turns round in Urdu and Arabic) creates the business.

**Step 2 — First store:** store name, a store code (auto-uppercased, e.g. `STR-001`), type (Retail Store or Warehouse), address line, city, postal code, country, timezone (matched to the supported countries). Submitting this creates the store, grants the Owner role, and lands the user on the Admin Dashboard.

**Flow:** Register → Business details → First store → Admin Dashboard. Adding *more* stores/zones or inviting staff happens afterward, from the Admin Console's Stores and Staff screens — it isn't part of the initial wizard.

**Alternate path — assisted onboarding:** a Platform Admin can set a tenant up on the business's behalf from the Platform Console's Tenants screen (see below) — a different, 2-step dialog (owner credentials, then business + store together) ending in an explicit "share these credentials with the owner" hand-off screen.

## 3. Platform Admin

**Persona:** the SaaS operator's own staff — monitors and manages the businesses (tenants) using the platform. Not a store/retail persona.

**Home — Platform Overview:** the signed-in admin's email under the title and a *Platform admin* badge (beside the heading from tablet width, under it on a phone); two cards — **Gateway**, which asks the gateway's public health check each time the page opens and says what it answered: *Healthy*, *Unhealthy* (it answered, and one of its checks is failing), *Unreachable*, or *Not readable here* when a web build's own server answers `/health` with the app's page instead of passing it on to the gateway — with **Check again**; and **Tenant management**, pointing at the Tenants screen — then *How tenant onboarding works*, in five numbered steps. It says only what it has read: there is no card claiming that every service is healthy, and no list of API routes (those are in [API-GUIDE.md](API-GUIDE.md), not on an operator's page). This dashboard reads more like an internal ops page than a customer-facing metrics dashboard — there are no tenant counts, growth charts, or usage metrics here (those live on the Tenants screen instead).

| Screen | What the user does |
|---|---|
| **Platform Overview** | See above. |
| **Tenants** | A table (wide) / card list (narrow) of every business on the platform: name, legal name, country, currency, status in words (*Active*, *Pending*, *Suspended*, *Deactivated*, *Closed*), created date, with the count under the title (*4 tenants*); on a phone the status and the action go on a line under the name. A business's sandbox (22.8) appears in the list as a tenant of its own with a **Sandbox** badge beside its status, so the platform never mistakes one for a customer. An **Activate** / **Deactivate** button on each tenant (with a confirmation explaining that a deactivated tenant and all its users immediately lose access — this is what triggers the storefront's "currently unavailable" notice). Pull to refresh on touch, **Refresh** for a mouse; an empty platform says *No tenants yet*. An **"Onboard new tenant"** button opens a 2-step dialog: create the owner's login, then the business + first store in one form; success shows a "tenant is live" confirmation with the owner's email to hand off. |
| **Plans** (21.8) | The price list, and the only place it is written. Each plan is a card: its name and code, how often it is billed, days free, whether it is on the public list, and a badge saying **Draft**, **On sale** or **Retired**; the one new businesses start on wears *New businesses start here* (below 600px the badges go under the name). Below it, **Price** — each as money from the day it took effect (*£29.00 from 1 Jan 2026*), or a warning in red that without one it cannot go on sale — and **What it includes** as chips. The actions follow what the plan can do: *Set a price*, *What it includes*, then *Put on sale* for a draft, and for one on sale *New businesses start here* and *Take off sale*. *Set a price* and *Price beyond the allowance* pre-fill no currency — the operator names it (*e.g. USD*), since the platform sells in more than one. **Write a plan** asks for the code, name, what it is for, how often it bills, days free and whether to list it; it is a draft until somebody sells it. *What it includes* offers only the keys the platform enforces, each labelled with the service that refuses when it is exceeded — a limit takes a number, or a dash for unlimited; a feature is a switch. **Metered use** (21.10) shows what the plan includes of each metered thing a period and what happens beyond it — *Orders taken: 1000 a period, then £0.05 each*, *Text messages: 50 a period, then marketing stops* — rows of text rather than chips, so a long line is never cut off, and a unit price set finer than the currency's minor unit keeps its places (*£0.035*). *Metered use* sets the allowances (a number, or a dash for unlimited), with a switch to stop marketing texts beyond the allowance rather than charge — offered for texts only, because an order is never refused; *Price beyond the allowance* sets what each one costs, per currency, to four places, from a date. Refusals are in words: *Give it a price before selling it*, *A plan already goes by that code*, *Orders are never refused. Price what is used beyond the allowance instead.* |
| **Billing** (21.9) | What the platform invoices as, and what is still owed. A card with the platform's legal name, country, invoice prefix, payment terms, tax rate and VAT number — or, in red, that the platform has not said who it invoices as, so nothing can be billed. **Owed**: how many businesses are suspended and the total still owed, one figure per currency (*£354.00 · €120.00*; pounds and euros do not add up), or *Every invoice is settled.* Then each unpaid invoice, most overdue first: its number, the business that owes it **by name** (from the invoice's own `tenantId`, looked up in the platform's tenants, one by one for a business past the list's first page — the invoice number alone, never an id, when no name can be read), *Due 1 Oct 2026* or, in the error colour, *Overdue since …*, where dunning has got to and what comes next (*reminder 1 · suspended next*), and the amount outstanding. Pull to refresh on touch, a refresh button for a mouse. |
| **Security incidents** | The register the Cyber Resilience Act and GDPR art.33(2) ask the platform to keep (`/platform/security`); what it is for sits under the title from tablet width and behind **What this register is for** on a phone. Filter *Open* / *Closed* / *All*; each incident shows its kind, when the platform became aware and its next deadline, with an *Open*, *Closed* or — in the error colour when a stage is past due — *Overdue* badge. Times are shown and chosen in the viewer's local time and travel in UTC: the zone is said once on each page (*Times are your local time (UTC+01:00).*), and a moment on the other side of a summer-time change carries its own (*24 Oct 2026 10:00 (UTC+01:00)*), because on a 24- or 72-hour deadline an hour's mislabel matters. **Open incident** takes the kind (actively exploited vulnerability, severe incident, personal data breach), a title, what happened, **Aware since** — a date and then a time from the pickers, never typed, now until changed and never later than now; every deadline runs from it — and the businesses affected: none chosen means every business, and the dialog will not submit while the business list cannot be loaded, so a failed read never widens an incident to everyone. An incident opens at its own address, `/platform/security/:id`, so a reload or a shared link lands on it and back — the browser's, or **All incidents** — returns to the register as it was left: its stages, each with what the law asks, its citation and *Due*, *Overdue since*, *Done*, *Waiting* or *No fixed time*; its timeline with each report's reference; and how many notices were issued and acknowledged. **Record** offers only the events that incident can still have, with **When** (blank is now; nothing later than now, nor before the platform became aware), the authority's reference and a note (required for a note); **Tell businesses** sends the notice once per business. Refusals (too early for the final report, a stage already recorded, closing with stages outstanding) are shown in the dialog as the server words them. Nothing is edited or deleted. |

**Flows:**
- *Monitor tenants:* Tenants list → inspect status → activate or deactivate.
- *Assisted onboarding:* Tenants screen → "Onboard new tenant" → owner credentials → business + first store → hand off.
- *Put a plan on sale:* Plans → Write a plan → Set a price → What it includes → Put on sale → (optionally) New businesses start here.
- *Chase what is owed:* Billing → the most overdue first, each by business → the dunning stage and what comes next.
- *Report an incident:* Security incidents → Open incident → record the early warning and the notification with the authority's references as they are made → the measure and the final report → Tell businesses → Close.

**Signing in with your business (20.x), web:** the sign-in screen has **Sign in with your business**, which asks for the business's sign-in name and sends the browser to the business's own identity provider; it comes back signed in, or on the sign-in screen with the reason in words (not added as staff yet, an address the provider has not verified, cancelled, took too long). A password the business no longer accepts says so and offers **Continue with <name>** in one press. A second step, when owed, follows exactly as after a password. Not offered in the native builds, which have no browser to send.

**Signing in with a second step (20.12), every shell:** after a right password, a login that holds a second factor lands on **One more step** (`/mfa`) — the six-digit code from its authenticator app, *Use a passkey* where the browser can (the web app; elsewhere the button is not shown), or *Lost your phone? Use a recovery code*. The card has one filled button, **Sign in**; the passkey button, offered first, is tonal. A wrong answer says so and keeps the step open; when the wait has ended it is the password again. A login that must have a second step and has none lands on **Set up a second step** (`/mfa/setup`): **Back to sign in** sits above the steps, then a QR code (drawn smaller in a short window, a laptop's 800px, so the steps stay above the fold) and the key to type by hand, the code that confirms it, then the ten recovery codes — and *Finish signing in* stays disabled until *I have kept these somewhere safe* is ticked. *Copy the key* and *Copy all* say *Copied*, with a tick, on the button itself for two seconds (read out by a screen reader), because in a dialog a snack bar would sit under the scrim. **Sign-in security** (`/account/security`; Account menu in Admin, the shield icon in POS and the platform console) is where a login sets up or removes its authenticator app, adds and removes passkeys by name (each with when it was last used, as a date), and makes new recovery codes; removal asks for the password, and is refused with an explanation — *A second step is required of this login, so the last one cannot be removed.*, in the info colour — when the business requires a second step and it is the last. A failed load shows the error with **Retry**.

**Notable features:** two independent, hard-separated login surfaces (platform vs. store) so credentials never cross over; deactivating a tenant immediately locks out its storefront and (implicitly) its admin/POS logins.

## 4. Tenant / Store Admin — the largest console

**Persona:** business owners, managers, and store staff. Covers catalog, inventory, purchasing, pricing, staff, stores/zones, and reporting for the whole business — all stores at once, not scoped to a single store.

**Home — Dashboard:** the page header says *Dashboard* with the business's name and currency under it, and a refresh button for a mouse (touch screens pull to refresh). A low-stock alert banner (up to 5 shown, "…and N more") when anything is at or below its threshold, each line by name — *Oat milk 1L (OAT-1L) at Leeds Road: 3 available, threshold 10* (the end of an id only while a name is unknown). Four stat cards, two to a row and all four in one row from 840px: **Revenue** and **Orders** cover the **last 30 days**, today included — reporting-svc's sales summary for the dashboard's own period, never whatever range the Reports screen was left on, the revenue net of refunds and with voided till sales already left out, in one currency (the business's own, or the one it sold in when it sold in nothing else; when it sold in more than one the caption names the currency both cards cover, *Last 30 days · GBP sales*, and Reports has the rest) — then **Low stock** and **SKUs**; a figure that could not be read shows *—*, never a zero. A horizontally-scrolling "Your stores" strip (name, code, warehouse vs. store icon, and the status in words beside a dot — *Active*, *Inactive*); and "Recent orders" (the channel icon for POS vs. Online, the order's reference, when it was placed — *Today, 14:05*, or the date for anything older — its status in words and its total) with **See all →** to the Orders screen.

Navigation (a labelled panel beside the content from 1200px, a drawer below it, *Find a page* with Ctrl+K / ⌘K; see §7.1): **Dashboard**, then *Sell* — **Orders · Fulfilment · Customers · Sales · Messages**; *Products & stock* — **Catalog · Inventory · Pricing · Procurement · Shelf space**; *Safety & compliance* — **Food safety · Recalls · Age checks · Legal obligations · Statutory returns**; *Money* — **Reports · Card machines · Card settlements · Chargebacks · Container deposits**; *Business* — **Stores · Staff · Plan · Integrations · Billing**; *Data & security* — **Audit · Privacy · Data retention · Data export · Security notices**. A storekeeper who is not also a manager sees only the pages their work needs.

### 4.1 Catalog (3 tabs)

| Tab | What the user does |
|---|---|
| **Products** | A search box and a category filter (on a phone the search takes the whole row and the category sits under it); the header counts what is loaded (*20+ products* while more remain). A table or card list showing name, description, category, Online/POS sellability, and the lifecycle state as a badge in words (4.2f); **Load more** fetches the next page, and a phone list is pulled to refresh. **New Product**: name, description, category, Online/POS sellability toggles, and an expandable **Safety information** section. Per-product actions: **View Variants**, **Sold at stores**, **Product image**, **Safety information**, **Delist**. Where EU product safety law binds the business, a banner names the online products that lack the information it requires, each with **Add**. |
| ↳ *Safety information* | What GPSR art.19 makes an online offer show: the manufacturer (name, postal address, e-mail or https link, two-letter country), the responsible person in the EU when the manufacturer is outside it, and the warnings — or *No warnings apply*. The dialog says whether this business's market requires it and what is still missing; a malformed contact or country is refused before sending, and the server's refusal (a product online cannot be left without it) is shown in place. |
| ↳ *Variants* | Add/view SKUs under a product: SKU code, barcode (with a camera scan button to fill it in), unit (e.g. "1kg"), and a **Set price** action per variant. Each variant shows its price on the default list as money in the business's currency (*No price* without one), *Active* or *Inactive*, and its EAN; on a phone the price goes under the SKU and *Set price* and *Allergens and origin* fold into one menu. Each variant also has **Allergens and origin**: a *Food product* switch; for food, the fourteen regulated allergens each marked **No / May contain / Contains** under a status banner (*Declared*, *Not yet declared — on the allergen gaps list*, or *Not yet marked as food*), and the ingredients as printed; country of origin and origin detail; the age-restriction category the till checks; and how the item is sold — each, weight, volume or length, catch weight, net content and its unit, and tare. **Saving never declares allergens by accident.** An empty declaration is a positive statement that the product contains none of the fourteen, so allergens are sent only after ticking *I have checked … against the label*, and changing any answer afterwards un-ticks it. Every compliance field goes on every save, because the update replaces them all and a field left out would be erased. |
| ↳ *Sold at stores* | Toggle "sell at all stores" (including future new stores) vs. hand-picking specific stores by checkbox — controls where a product shows up on POS/storefront. |
| ↳ *Product image* | Upload (JPEG/PNG/WebP, size-limited) or remove the image shown on the storefront listing and product page. |
| **Categories** | A table of name, parent and status (as a badge in words), with one level of parent/child nesting and every row reachable by scrolling either way. Create/Edit: name + optional parent. **Edit** and **Deactivate** sit in each row's menu; deactivating hides a category from product-assignment dropdowns without deleting it. |
| **Import** | Upload a supplier catalogue CSV; the app parses it client-side and previews row count, distinct products/categories/stores detected, and whether price/quantity columns are present — before anything is submitted. Lets the admin map each CSV store name to a real store and pick one destination store to receive stock into, in either **"add"** or **"replace existing"** mode. A built-in "expected format" help dialog documents the CSV schema. One import creates categories, products, prices, and stock receipts together, then reports created counts and itemized errors per category (catalog / stock-receipt / price). |

### 4.2 Inventory (10 tabs)

| Tab | What the user does |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | Search by product, SKU or ID and a **Low stock** filter chip (uses configured reorder thresholds when set). A table of each product (with its SKU) at each store by name — On-Hand, Reserved, Available and a *Low (≤10)* / *OK* badge — or, on a phone, a card per product and store with *Avail: 12 · On-hand: 15 · Reserved: 3* under the name; infinite scroll. The search runs over the rows loaded so far and says so (*Only the items loaded so far are searched.*); finding nothing says what was looked for (*No items match “teapot”*, *No items are low on stock*) with **Clear filters**. Per-row actions in a ⋮ menu: **Adjust stock** (signed delta + reason) and **Set reorder level**. |
| **Batches** | Pick a store (*Choose a store* until one is), then filter by zone and material status — *Any status*, *Available*, *In quarantine*, *On hold*, *Rejected*, the same words the badges and the change dialog use — and search by batch or product. Each batch by its product's name and SKU, with batch number, what remains, zone by name (*Unassigned* when it has none), expiry as a date, grade and material status as a badge; below the table's width, cards (*Zone: A1 · Expires 4 Oct 2026*, *12 of 24 left*) that scroll with the filters. **Change material status** per batch; a banner for batches expiring within 30 days (*2 batches expire within 30 days — e.g. B-104, on 4 Oct 2026*). |
| **Reduce to clear** | The morning's job on a fresh counter (05.4, 03.9). Pick a store and a horizon (3/7/14/30 days): **To sticker** lists every batch expiring within it — product, batch, days to go, packs left, the current POS price and what the ladder says (*£4.00 → £2.40 (40 % off, 2-day step)*, or *no step on the ladder yet*, or *no POS price to reduce from*); when inventory cannot be read the list says so instead of showing nothing to do. **Sticker** opens the batch with the ladder's step and the whole batch filled in; the counter keeps or changes the packs, gives a percentage off *or* a price (not both), and a reason (short dated, clearance, damaged pack, overstock); the server prices it and answers with the **code to print on the stickers** — thirteen digits the till scans — shown large, with the price and what it was. A batch already stickered shows its code instead of the button. **Stickered** lists the store's markdowns (active / expired / taken off / all), each by its product's name with the price and what it was, *n of m left* as the till sells them, the expiry and reason, and its status as a badge (*Active*, *Expired*, *Taken off*), and **Take off** cancels one with a reason: the code stops scanning, what sold stays sold. **Ladder** edits the steps (days to expiry → % off) for the whole business or, with the switch, this store only; a manager's action — the tab reads it for everyone. |

- A list of stores/warehouses: name, code, city/country (a warehouse and a dark store each with their own icon), "Prices shown" vs. **Catalog mode**, and the status in words — *Open* while it trades, *Closed* when switched off. In a wide window each row carries **Zones**, **Instruments** and **Delivery** and a labelled Open/Closed switch; narrower, or at large text, the row keeps the name, the details and the status, and everything else — **Edit store**, Zones, Instruments, Delivery, and **Close store** / **Open store** — is in one ⋮ menu.
- **Add Store**: name, code, type (Retail Store / Warehouse / **Dark store (online only)** — a shop with no shop floor that fills online orders for delivery only: the storefront lists it delivery-only, no collection is offered there and no till opens; its row reads *Dark store · delivery only, no till*), address, country, timezone, and a **"Show prices on storefront"** toggle (turning it off puts the store into Catalog mode — see [§7](#7-cross-cutting-ux--product-features)).
- **Edit Store**: the code and type shown, fixed since creation, the type in words (*STR-001 · Shop*, *Warehouse*, *Dark store*), then the same fields plus **accepted payment methods** (Cash / Card / UPI / Wallet — at least one must stay enabled; this drives what's offered at POS tender and storefront checkout) and **At the till, ask for the customer's phone** — *Required* / *Optional* / *Don't ask*, *Optional* until an owner or manager chooses (on **Add Store** too), with the line *Used to warn the buyer if something they bought is recalled. Optional lets the customer say no.* ([a phone at the till](../intent/phone-at-the-till.md)).
- **Zones dialog** (per store): list of zones/aisles, each with its code and type in words and its status as a badge; add/edit a zone's name, code, and type (Aisle, Rack, Shelf, Cold Room, Back Store, Receiving, Display) — the store's internal location map — with **Edit** and **Deactivate** / **Activate** (one menu on a phone).
- **Delivery** (per store) — the pincodes (postcodes) the store fulfils for home delivery, each with a priority; where several stores cover the same one, the lower priority wins.
- **Delivery & collection slots** (per store; owners and managers, a manager held to stores only at theirs) — the windows the store offers, delivery and collection apart, by weekday: *17:00–19:00*, *Capacity 12 · cut-off 120 min before*, switched on or off. **Add window** / **Edit window** take the weekday, from and to (24-hour pickers), how many orders it takes and how long before it starts orders stop; the times are the store's own (*Times are the store's own: Europe/Warsaw*). An overlapping or back-to-front window is refused in words. A store with no windows checks out as before.
- **Close store** asks first — *It stops taking online orders and till sales until it is opened again. Nothing is deleted.* — and the snack bar says *Leeds Road is closed.*; **Open store** puts it back.
- **Instruments** (per store) — the weighing-instrument register (Weights and Measures Act 1985). Every scale the store weighs for trade on, with its standing derived from its history — *Certified for trade*, *Never verified*, *Failed its last check*, *Repaired since last verified*, *Re-verification overdue*, *Out of service*, *Retired* — each scale by its identifier and kind in words (*counter scale*, *labelling scale*, *platform scale*, *hanging scale*) with its next verification *due* as a date, and, for a manager, **Register scale**, **Record verification…** (initial verification, re-verification, trading-standards inspection, or a repair, which is never a pass and takes the scale out of trade until it is verified again), **History**, **Edit**, and the status actions. Retirement asks for confirmation and is final. A labelling scale carries the label scheme the till reads its barcodes with. Until a scale is registered and verified, the till cannot sell anything by weight at that store.


### 4.4 Orders

- One list covering **both channels** (Online and POS), with channel chips (*All channels*, *Online*, *Till*) and status chips (*All statuses*, *Awaiting price*, *Pending*, *Confirmed*, *Part fulfilled*, *Fulfilled*, *Cancelled*, *Voided*) and infinite scroll; a touch screen pulls to refresh, a mouse gets a refresh button. Each row: the order's reference, a *Till* or *Online* badge, how the customer said they'd pay (*Cash on delivery*, *Cash at pickup*, *Card*, *UPI*, *Wallet*), when it was placed, its status in words and its total — on a phone the reference and the total share the first line and the badges get a line of their own.
- An online order placed in a delivery or collection window says it on its row and in its detail — *Delivery · Sat 27 Sep, 17:00–19:00* — in the store's own time, as the server wrote it.
- Per-order actions: **Confirm**, **Mark fulfilled**, **Collect payment**, **Cancel**, **Return / Refund**, **Print receipt**. **Price order** appears for an *Awaiting price* catalog-mode till order (SJ-D41): a unit price per line (each line by its product's name) and the VAT, then the order is released as pending. *Mark fulfilled* opens **Picked & packed** (SJ-D35; ship-from-store): each line by its product's name and SKU with what is still outstanding (*2 of 3 outstanding*) and a quantity picked now — leave them as they are to pick everything, lower one to pick part; the handover to the shopper or the carrier is recorded on the Fulfilment screen; the order shows **Part fulfilled** (a filter of its own) until every line is complete, and *Mark fulfilled* is offered again for the rest. More than is outstanding is stopped in the dialog, and the server's refusal is shown in words. A till sale arrives here already **Fulfilled** — it is handed over when it is paid for — so *Mark fulfilled* is only ever offered for online and delivery orders. **Void sale** (09.13) is offered on a till sale that is still standing, to an owner or manager only: the dialog says up front that the stock goes back and the receipt keeps its number, asks for a reason and will not send without one, and shows the server's refusal in words — an online order is cancelled or returned instead, a voided sale cannot be voided twice, and anyone below manager is refused before the sale is touched. A voided sale is filtered under **Voided**, counted against the manager on the **Staff Exceptions** report, and left out of the sales reports and the Dashboard's revenue (reporting-svc hears `OrderVoided`).
- **Collect payment**: for orders where money wasn't captured online (cash-on-delivery/at-pickup, or a Catalog-mode order) — shows the order total, what has already been collected and what is outstanding (or *This order is already paid in full.*), and lets staff pick the tender — *Cash*, *Card*, *UPI* or *Wallet*, the customer's declared one picked already — and capture the balance at handover: *£42.30 collected in cash.*
- **Return / Refund**: pick quantities to return per line (each by its product's name, SKU, what was ordered and its price), a reason, and a refund method (card/original tender, cash, or store credit); a live refund-total preview; posts the refund automatically against the original payment, or credits store credit. The dialog lists **Previous returns** with their refund as money and method (*· £4.20 via Store credit (Completed)*), and the confirmation reads *Return recorded · £4.20 refunded* (or *as store credit*).
- **Cancel order**, with confirmation.

### 4.4a Fulfilment (`/admin/fulfilment`) — ship-from-store and dark-store picking

One store's online orders as work, for storekeepers and managers (a store dropdown at the top: shops and dark stores, a dark store named as one; never a warehouse). **To pick** counts the confirmed orders waiting at the store (picked on Inventory › Picking & putaway, as a wave or one order at a time). Each order in the queue is *Order #* and the end of its id, its total and when it was placed. **Packed — awaiting courier** lists the picked deliveries, each with **Dispatch**: the carrier (required), its reference or tracking number and the parcel count (both optional) — *Dispatched with DPD.* **Ready for collection** lists the picked pickups, each with **Collected**: who took it (optional). **Handed over today** lists what left since the store's own midnight, whenever the order was placed (`handedFrom=`, the handover's time — yesterday's delivery dispatched this morning is among today's), reading *Dispatched · DPD 1Z…* or *Collected by Sam*. A pick leaves an order FULFILLED — the Orders list's dialog now reads **Picked & packed** (*How much of each line is picked and packed now*) — and the handover is recorded here, once; the server's refusals (not yet picked, the wrong kind, handed over already, another store's order) are shown in words. The packed and ready queues come **in window order** (orders without a window last), each order with its window in the store's time; *handed over today* stays in the order it was handed over.

**Outstanding lines** (substitutions for out-of-stock online lines) sits between *To pick* and the queue: the store's confirmed and part-picked online orders with each line's name and *2 of 3 outstanding*, and whether the shopper allows substitutions; on a phone **Substitute** and **Short** go under the line so the product's name keeps the width. Per line, **Substitute** (only where the shopper allowed it) opens the stand-ins the business declared, each with its SKU and what the shelf has — tap one, or type the variant id of what was packed — the quantity (what the line still owes, at most) and an optional reason: *Substituted. The shopper is told and pays no more.* **Short** takes the quantity (what is owed, at most) and a reason: *Closed short. The shopper is told and refunded.* Both are sent once per press (an idempotency key), and the server's refusals — the shopper said no, none on the shelf, no price at the store, more than is owed, another store's order — are shown in words. An order whose every line is picked or closed leaves the list and appears packed or ready.

### 4.7 Reports

A sidebar (wide) / a sideways-scrolling row of chips (narrow — the selected report always scrolled into view, so the row never hides which report is showing) between nineteen reports, each with a refresh button and, where it has rows, **Export CSV**. Reports that cover a period carry *From* / *To* date chips (*From: 26 Aug 2026*) and name the period in their caption; those that group carry grouping chips. The sidebar scrolls — the list outgrew a laptop window, and a plain column does not merely hide the overflow but makes it unreachable. **Names, not ids:** a store by its name, a product by its name with its SKU, a member of staff by their login email (iam-svc's `GET /auth/admin/staff-users?ids=`, asked about once per person while the screen is open; *System* for stock the platform moved itself, *Unattributed* for an entry naming no one), a reason, a tender or a method in words — the end of an id only while a name is unknown. **Amounts are money:** in the row's own currency where the report carries one, else in the business's home currency.

*Business-wide, from reporting-svc:*

1. **On-Hand Inventory** — units on hand per product (with its SKU) at each store, headed *42 SKUs · 1380 units total* (a product stocked at two stores is two rows but one SKU).
2. **Sales Revenue** — orders, gross, refunded, and net revenue by currency. A till sale voided after the fact is left out — reporting-svc hears order-svc's `OrderVoided` — and its refunds with it. Date range.
3. **Sales by Day** — daily revenue buckets; a voided sale is left out, as in Sales Revenue. Date range.
3a. **Sales by Category** — what each category took: orders, units, gross and share, by the product's own category or rolled up to the top of the tree (a segmented toggle). Names come from the catalogue; lines the catalogue cannot place are shown as *Uncategorised*, a category it no longer lists by the end of its id; a voided sale's lines are left out. Date range, CSV export.
4. **Supply / Demand Netting** — on-hand + in-transit → net available, per store/variant.
5. **Movement Statistics** — stock in / out / net per period, per store/variant.

*From the service that owns the data:*

6. **Low Stock** (inventory-svc) — items below their own reorder level, worst shortfall first. Names the **signal** that bound each row (THRESHOLD / SAFETY_STOCK / REORDER_POINT), so a manager can see *why* an item is flagged and therefore what to change.
7. **Stock Valuation** (inventory-svc) — holding value on the configured FIFO or AVERAGE basis, grouped by store or variant. Stock carrying no cost is reported **separately and excluded** from the value rather than counted as zero, which would understate the holding; the screen says so when there is any.
8. **Shrinkage** (inventory-svc) — stock written off and found over a period, grouped by reason, staff member or store. Write-offs and finds are shown as separate columns on purpose: a store that wrote off 100 units and found 100 others is not a store that did nothing, and a net figure alone would say it was. Date range.
9. **Staff Exceptions** (order-svc) — loss prevention's view: discounts granted, sales voided and no-sale drawer opens over a period, grouped by staff member (by login email) or store, most exceptions first. Each row carries the staff member's journalled sales so the counts read as a rate (`per 100`) rather than a ranking of who worked the most shifts; when nothing journalled a sale the rate shows `—` and the screen says why, because a zero there would read as "impeccably behaved". Exceptions recorded with no actor are shown as **Unattributed** rather than dropped. Date range.
10. **Tax Summary** (pricing-svc) — net / VAT / gross by rate band, store or month, reconciling to VAT return boxes 1 and 6. Exempt lines are marked, because the return counts their net in Box 6 and their VAT in no box at all. When Box 1 and total VAT disagree an exempt line is carrying VAT — the screen warns before the return is filed rather than dropping it silently. Date range (**required** here; pricing-svc rejects the call without one).
11. **Sales by Hour** (order-svc) — takings bucketed by hour of the trading day with a bar per hour, so a manager can staff to the actual peak rather than to a guess. Counted on the **browser's own clock**: the client sends its timezone, because the server stores UTC and a shop outside it would otherwise be told it is busiest at the wrong time of day. Channel chips compare the till against the website. Hours with no trade produce no row, and the screen says how many of the 24 are absent rather than letting a short table read as a quiet day. Date range.
12. **Sales by Staff** (order-svc) — what each cashier (by login email) rang up, their average basket, and their discount rate as a share of the *undiscounted* ticket. Read from the POS transaction journal, so **in-store only** — a note on the screen says so, because a manager comparing it against Sales Revenue will otherwise be chasing the online orders that have no cashier to attribute. Entries naming nobody show as **Unattributed** rather than being dropped. Date range.
13. **Tender Mix** (payment-svc) — how the take split across cash, card, gift card and the rest, with each method's share of the net. Refunds are subtracted **within their own method**: a card sale refunded to store credit is not a zero-card day, and the split is what reconciles against a merchant statement. Failed tenders are counted in their own column and called out above the table, because a method whose declines climb against healthy volume is a terminal problem no sales report would show. Date range.
14. **Stock Turn** (inventory-svc) — cost of goods sold against the average value held to produce it, with turns and days-on-hand, slowest first. Two separate caveats appear when they apply and mean different things: that the movement ledger was archived past the window's start, so opening values are a floor; and that some quantity sold out of batches with no cost price, excluded from COGS rather than costed at zero. Turns show `—` rather than `0` where there was nothing to turn — a different finding. Date range (**required**; the endpoint rejects the call without one).
15. **Gross Margin** (inventory-svc, 19.7) — what the sales earned, net of VAT, discounts and returns, against the cost of the batches they drew down, per store or per product, with margin %, GMROI over the window and per year, lowest margin first. The header carries the total margin and its share of revenue. A loss is shown in the error colour, not hidden behind a zero. Margin % and GMROI show `—` where nothing was earned or nothing was held. Amounts are money in the business's home currency — inventory-svc records what order-svc sent and never learns a currency, so the report carries none and the app names the business's own. Three caveats appear when they apply, and each says which way it moves the figure: archived history (average holdings are a floor, GMROI reads high); units sold with **no revenue recorded**, from sales made before orders carried it (their cost is still counted, so the margin reads low); units sold out of batches with **no cost price** (left out of COGS, so the margin reads high). Date range (**required**).
16. **Dead Stock** (inventory-svc) — stock on hand aged into the 0-30 / 31-60 / 61-90 / 91-180 / 180+ ladder, with the value sitting in each band and the total at risk in the header. A **Since** column names which date each age is measured from — "last sale", or "received" where the line has never sold — because 400 days since receipt and 400 days since a sale are not the same claim. No date bar: dead stock is a question about now, not about a period.
17. **Trial Balance** (purchase-svc) — every nominal code's debits, credits and balance over the range, from the ledger the documents write: goods receipts (stock against the accrual), supplier invoices (the creditor), credit notes, rejections, intercompany invoices and manual journals. Totals at the foot; when they disagree the screen warns that the ledger does not balance and says it is a fault to investigate rather than printing two totals in the same font. Date range (plain dates: this endpoint takes days, not instants). **Post journal** opens a dialog for a manual journal — date, description, two to fifty lines of code / account / debit / credit — with the running difference on screen and the Post button disabled until it reads 0.00, so a finance user typing twenty lines does not learn on the twenty-first that the third was wrong; a refusal from the server (a closed period, say) is shown in words with the journal still open. **Sales clearing** (17.7) sits above the table: sales now reach the ledger from the till and the storefront — each tender to its control account, the confirmed sale to sales and VAT output through 1105 Sales Receipts Clearing — so a sale paid in full clears to zero. When every sale's takings cleared the card says so in one line; otherwise it reads *Open sales clearing: n order(s)* and expands to each order with its balance, whether money was taken with no confirmed sale or a sale was confirmed for more than was taken, and the day it was first posted, for finance to resolve before closing the period.
18. **Deferred Revenue** (purchase-svc, 17.11) — loyalty points and gift cards on the ledger under FRS 102 section 23. **Estimates**: what a point is worth (to its own places, *£0.005*), the share of points never spent and of gift card value never claimed, and why; until they are set the report says how many loyalty events are waiting, because nothing is deferred without them. **Loyalty points**: outstanding, the deferred income (2330), and points spent before their earning arrived. **Gift cards**: loaded, spent, breakage recognised (4031) and the liability left. **Set estimates** / **Change estimates** (a change applies from now on, and the server posts the events that waited). No date bar.

### 4.8 Customers

A phone is found however it is typed in the business's own countries — national form, international form, spaces or none (*512 345 678* at a Polish shop finds *+48 512 345 678*) — never another business's customer.

While Marketing stands withdrawn the channels are off and cannot be switched on (*Switch on Marketing first*); withdrawing Marketing switches every channel off at once. Where the business's privacy law asks for consent per purpose, Marketing must be granted before any channel.
- **Addresses**: add/edit saved addresses tagged Home/Work/Billing/Shipping, with a "Default" indicator.
- **Export data** — a subject access / portability request (art.15/20) that reached the shop by phone or letter: the same document the storefront's *Download my data* produces, copied to the clipboard and shown. All of it or none of it — if order-svc cannot be reached nothing partial is produced.
- **Anonymize** (with confirmation) — a GDPR-style right-to-be-forgotten control that scrubs the customer's personal details and their saved addresses, and tells order-svc/notification-svc to redact what each holds about them (SJ-D43): a settled order is redacted at once, an order still being fulfilled once it finishes.

- **Send text** (13.7) on a customer with a phone number: a text through notification-svc's SMS channel to the E.164 number on the record — transactional by default, or marketing, which the server allows only with the customer's recorded SMS consent and refuses in words otherwise. The body is capped at 1600 characters before it is sent.

### 4.10 Staff

Two tabs: **People** and **Roles** (20.10).

- **Single sign-on** (20.x) — a button on the People tab opens the business's identity provider: the sign-in name staff type, the issuer, the client id and secret (never shown again once saved; left empty, the saved one is kept), whether it is switched on, whether a first sign-in is matched by email only when the provider verified it, and which of Managers, Storekeepers and Cashiers may no longer use a password — owners always can. It shows the redirect URI to register with the provider, **Check** reads the provider now and lists what holds and what does not, and **Disconnect** turns it off after a confirmation. Only an owner saves; a manager can look.
- **Second step** (20.12) — a button on the People tab opens the business's rule: which of Owners, Managers, Storekeepers and Cashiers must sign in with a second step as well as a password; only an owner saves it. Each row of the staff list has **Reset second step (lost phone)**, which clears that person's authenticator app, passkeys and recovery codes and signs them out everywhere, after a confirmation that says so.

On a phone **Reset second step** and **Remove** fold into one ⋮ menu. **Remove** (with confirmation: *Remove Cashier access for sam@shop.test at Leeds Road. They lose access to it immediately.*) — and the role comes off the login, not just the list (SJ-D51). A manager held to stores sees the people at those stores and the business-wide ones (the owner) — names and assignments alike; the owner sees everyone.
- **Assign Staff**: enter an email (finds an existing account or provisions a new one), optional password, a role — the four built-in ones, then **the tenant's own roles**, each named with the tier it stands on — and a store. An existing login is confirmed with *sam@shop.test assigned as Cashier.*; if a new account was created, a one-time **"Staff account created"** dialog reveals a temporary password (masked by default, tap-to-reveal, one-tap copy with a "clear your clipboard" reminder).
- **Roles**: the four built-in tiers, each saying what it holds, beside the tenant's own, each by its name with the tier it stands on (*Shift lead · on Manager*, *On Manager · holds sales.void, …*) and a *Custom* badge — never a role's code alone. **Define role**: a code (upper snake case; a built-in name is refused before it is sent), a name, the tier it stands on, and **the permissions it holds — the checkboxes offered are exactly the tier's**, because the server refuses anything outside them and a box that cannot be ticked should not be on the screen; changing the tier drops the ticks the new tier cannot hold. **Redefine** keeps the code and tier fixed; the snackbar says holders carry the change from their next sign-in. **Delete** is refused in words while anyone still holds the role.
- What a login's permissions do on screen: **Void sale**, **Approve / Reject** on a flagged invoice are offered only to a login that holds `sales.void` / `purchasing.invoices.decide` (an owner always; a manager by default; a custom role by its claim). The server refuses the rest regardless — the app only hides what would fail.
- Also reachable from every Admin screen's app bar: **Change password** (the published password rule shown before typing; a wrong current password says so, and a new password the policy refuses says why) and **Sign out**.

**Key admin flows:**
- *New product to sellable:* Catalog → New Product → add a Variant (SKU/barcode/unit) → Set price → (optional) restrict "Sold at stores" → product image → now visible on POS/storefront.
- *Bulk catalog setup:* Catalog → Import → upload CSV → review parsed preview → map store names → choose destination store → import → review per-category error report.
- *Purchase-to-shelf:* Procurement → Create PO → add lines with cost/tax → Submit → (goods arrive) → Receive goods → stock levels update, visible immediately in Inventory.
- *Order lifecycle (either channel):* Orders → Confirm → Mark fulfilled / Collect payment (if COD) → optionally Return/Refund later.
- *Staffing a new store:* Stores → Add Store (+ Zones) → Staff → Assign Staff (role + store) → temp password handed to the new hire.

**Notable UX/product features:** barcode camera scanning reused in Products (variant barcode) and Inventory (receive stock); a consistent responsive wide-table/narrow-card-list pattern across every list screen; consistent "Delist"/"Deactivate"/"Remove" terminology per entity type; per-store **payment-method** and **Catalog-mode** toggles that ripple into POS and Storefront; a few sensitive actions (special-order creation, payment collection) are safe to retry without double-effect.

### 4.11 Audit trail (management)

The business audit trail (20.11): who discounted, voided, opened the drawer without a sale, cancelled an order, took goods back or wrote stock off — one timeline, newest first, each row naming the member of staff. Two sources: order-svc's `GET /admin/audit/events` (discounts, voids, no-sales, cancels, returns — every filter server-side, cursor-paged, *Load older* fetches the next page) and inventory-svc's movements ledger read as `type=ADJUST` only (a single bounded read; the period and the actor are applied on the client because that ledger filters by store and type alone). Filters: one store or all, a date range (the last 30 days by default), one kind of action or everything, one member of staff or anyone — inline from tablet width, behind one *Filters · n* button on a phone so the trail starts on the first screen. Each row shows the action with its own qualifier in words (the discount and the role that authorised it, *the order was confirmed* for a cancel, *£4.20 refunded to the original payment* for a return, *authorised by* the supervisor for a no-sale, an adjustment's signed quantity and product), when, who — by login email (iam-svc names every person the trail and the staff list mention, someone who has since left included; *Unattributed* when the log recorded nobody) — the order and the reason (a stockroom reason code in words: *Damaged*, *Found during a count*, *Theft or shrinkage*, *Expired*, *Returned to the supplier*, *Correction*, *Quality sampling*), with a *Till* or *Stock* chip for its source. **Export CSV** writes what is loaded. Management-only nav item, after Reports.

## 5. POS — cashier-facing terminal

**Persona:** in-store cashiers (and managers acting as cashiers). Built to work as a real till on a tablet or desktop, and to degrade sensibly on a phone.

**Entry point — Clock-in:** the whole terminal is gated behind clocking in — a cashier picks a store/terminal before any sale can start; a background heartbeat keeps the session alive while clocked in, and "Clock out" is one tap away. Its confirmation says what happens to the work in hand: *Any sale in progress is kept*, or, with sales still queued, how many haven't reached the server — *they stay on this till and are sent when the network is back.* **Pending is the exception to the gate:** sales taken offline are money the server has not been told about, so the Pending screen stays reachable, and syncable, while clocked out, and the clock-in card offers *2 sales waiting to sync* whenever any are. Once clocked in, the default landing is the Sale screen.

Navigation: **Sale · Tender · Cash · Pending** (a distinct amber accent branding sets the POS apart from the Admin Console's look). *Pending* carries a badge with the number of sales the server has not accepted yet, so an unsynced sale is visible from anywhere in the terminal. The app bar holds **Customer display** (web), **Returns**, **Receipt printer**, **Sign-in security**, **Clock out** and **Sign out** — every one on the bar from tablet width; on a phone only Returns stays and the rest go into ⋮, so the title keeps its room.

| Screen | What the user does |
|---|---|
| **Sale** | From 720px of content width (a tablet in portrait, and anything wider): a persistent two-pane layout — product catalog on the start side (search, in-stock filter, category chips, tap-to-add), the running sale on the end side (360–420px). On phones: the sale view only, with a **Browse products** icon button opening the same catalog as a bottom sheet, and the totals folded into one row that expands on tap. In a short window — a phone on its side, wide enough for both panes but leaving the sale under 300px of height — the panes stack and the rows above the sale's lines scroll with them, the totals staying pinned. On a keyboard till **F2** puts the cursor in the barcode field from anywhere on the screen, and the field shows an *F2* hint (*Scan barcode or type SKU…*; *Scan or type SKU…* on a phone). A barcode field accepts a camera scan, a handheld scanner, or manual entry. Each line supports qty +/- and swipe-to-remove. A customer bar attaches a registered customer or records a walk-in's phone number, as the store's till choice says ([a phone at the till](../intent/phone-at-the-till.md)): *Customer phone \** — *This store asks for a number on every sale* — under **Required**, where the sale waits for a number or a customer; *Customer phone (optional)* — *Leave blank if the customer prefers not to say* — under **Optional**; no phone field under **Don't ask**. Actions: **Hold** (park the sale, with a discard-confirmation if resuming over unsaved changes), **Resume** (pick from held sales), **No sale** (open the drawer without a transaction, logged for loss-prevention). A totals bar shows subtotal, an optional order-level discount, the container deposit as its own row, and net total, with **Charge £12.40**; folded on a phone or in a short window, it is one row — *Total* with what is in it (*3 items · £1.00 off · £0.30 deposit*) — that a tap opens (*Show price breakdown*). |
| **Tender** | **Split/multi-tender payment**: *Total due*, *Paid* and *Remaining* (in the same ink as the total — an unpaid balance is the normal state while tendering, not an error — then *Change* once it is settled); add one or more payments until the balance clears — Cash, Card, UPI and Wallet only where the owner enabled them for the store, Gift card and Store credit always; cash/card entries offer quick preset amounts or "Exact," cash shows change due; gift-card/store-credit entries look up and cap against the actual balance (*Balance: £20.00 · Applies: £12.40*). Each tender added shows its method, the amount and, for cash, *Given £20.00 · change £7.60* (a gift card its code), and can be removed before completing. **The customer's phone** is on this screen too whenever no customer is attached and the store's till asks for one ([a phone at the till](../intent/phone-at-the-till.md)) — the same value as the sale's customer bar, typed in either: under *Required* **Complete Sale** waits for it and says why at the field (*Enter the customer's number, or attach the customer*); under *Optional* it may be left blank; under *Don't ask* there is none. A number that is no phone where the business trades comes back at the field — *That isn't a phone number where this business trades — check it, or leave it blank* — with the sale kept. On a phone the figures, the payment buttons and the tenders scroll as one column and **Complete Sale** stays in a bar at the foot of the screen; wider, it is a form-width column. On completion: a "Sale complete" confirmation with order number and change due, plus **Reprint** and **Email** receipt actions, then "New sale." (The Email action records the receipt + address against the order and reports success, but no email is actually dispatched today — there's no receipt consumer behind it.) Completing a sale hands it over: the order is fulfilled when the last tender lands, so its stock is deducted without anyone opening it afterwards. Until SJ-D40 every till sale waited at *Confirmed* for a manager to click *Mark fulfilled*, and its stock never moved if nobody did. The receipt carries the **legal receipt number** (e.g. `2026-000042`): the number is issued a few seconds after the last tender, so the till asks order-svc to hold the request while the number is issued (`?wait=12`, one round trip in the common case) and retries briefly after, rather than polling sixteen times and giving up. If it has not arrived, the receipt says so instead of printing a number, and *Reprint* fetches it again. An offline sale's receipt says its number is issued when the sale reaches the server — and once it does, the **Offline sales** screen lists the synced sale under the same reference the paper receipt carries, with the receipt number the server issued on replay (looked up when the screen is opened, remembered once found, *Check again* if it is not issued yet). (The receipt used to print "Receipt #" over the first eight characters of the order's id — an order reference wearing a receipt number's label; it is now labelled *Order ref*.) |
| **Customer display** (app bar, web) | Opens a second window for the customer's side of the counter (`#/pos/display`, no till chrome, large type). It shows the store's name while idle, and heads the sale and the thank-you with it too, so the top of the screen says whose till this is; the sale as it is rung up — each line as it is scanned, the subtotal, any discount, the refundable container deposit as its own row, the total, every amount as money the way the back office writes it (*£5.50*); then **Thank you** with what was paid and the change due, held for a few seconds over the till clearing its cart until the next customer's first line. The till and the display talk over a browser BroadcastChannel (same origin, same browser, a second monitor); where there is no second window to reach, the action is not offered. |
| **Returns** (app bar) | **Container return (09.16)**: empties brought back are typed by material, volume and count; the scheme in force where the store trades prices them as they are typed (**Pay back €0.75**), a container outside its band counts for nothing, and **Refund** records the pay-out against the clocked-in till session once (*Deposit refunded: hand back €0.75*). A store with no scheme in force says so and pays nothing. On the sale, a scanned drink whose container the scheme takes back carries its deposit as **Container deposit (refundable)** — its own row under the subtotal, added to what is due, never discounted — and the receipt prints it as its own line. |
| **Tender** *(Catalog-mode store)* | Skips payment entirely when the clocked-in store has prices hidden: lists the items and a single **Place order** button — no prices, no tender. The order is placed **awaiting a price** (SJ-D41 closed): it shows in Admin → Orders as *Awaiting price*, is never cancelled by the stranded-order sweeper, and a manager's **Price order** action gives each line its unit price and the VAT, after which it is an ordinary pending order to collect payment on. |
| **Cash / Till** | The open till is the server's, not the device's. Each time the screen opens, and whenever the store changes, it asks payment-svc for the till this cashier opened at this store and has not closed (`GET /admin/cash/till-sessions/current`; *Checking the till…*), so after a reload, or on another device at the same counter, it carries on with that till rather than offering a second. If the server cannot say, **Open till** stays, with the reason — *Couldn't check whether a till is already open here.* — and **Try again**: no guess either way, and no retry behind the cashier's back. **Open till**: enter a starting cash float, in the business's currency (its symbol before the field, never a dollar sign on a pound till). Once open, the **Till session** card (*#* and the end of its id) shows the opening float, gross sales, refunds, net sales, cash drops and **expected cash in till**, refreshed by a pull or the refresh button; **Cash drop** (amount + notes), **Pay in** and **Pay out** (amount + reason), and **Close till (Z-report)** — shows expected cash, takes the counted cash, and confirms *Till closed* with *Balanced — no discrepancy.* or *Over by* / *Short by* the difference. |
| **Pending** | Sales taken while the server was unreachable, held on the till until it accepts them. A banner says how many are not yet on the server and how many need a person (*3 sales not yet on the server · 1 need attention*, turning to the error container while a refused sale waits), with **Sync now**. Each row shows the sale reference printed on the customer's receipt (the end of its id, in capitals), the total as money, the number of items, when it was taken, and how many attempts it has had. Waiting sales retry on their own; a sale the server has *refused* is parked with the reason and offers **Try again** and **Discard** (confirmed, because the customer has already paid). With nothing waiting: *Everything is synced*. Reachable while clocked out. |
| **Receipt printer** *(app-bar action)* | How this till produces a receipt (09.12), kept on the device because the printer is hardware on one till: the browser's print dialog (the web default), a **network receipt printer** over ESC/POS on port 9100 (desktop and mobile tills), a **print bridge** on the LAN that takes ESC/POS bytes (the way a browser till reaches thermal paper), a **file** (downloaded on the web, written to the documents folder on a native till), or none. Host and port or the bridge address are validated before anything is saved; paper is 58 or 80 mm; *Open the cash drawer on a cash sale* kicks the drawer connected to the printer on a cash tender and never on a reprint. **Print test page** proves the path before a customer is waiting. Every receipt the till prints — after a sale, a reprint, the offline copy — goes the way these settings say, the outcome is shown in words when it is not the browser dialog, and a failed print says why rather than throwing. **Save** on the sale-complete dialog keeps a copy whatever the mode. The ESC/POS document strips control characters from every text field, so a product name can never open the drawer or cut the paper. The record kept against the order says how the receipt came out: `PRINT`, `THERMAL`, `SAVE` or `EMAIL`. |

**Flows:**
- *Start of shift:* Clock in (pick store — a dark store is never offered: it has no till) → Sale screen unlocked → Cash: open a till, or carry on with the one this cashier left open at the store.
- *Ring up a sale:* scan/search items → attach a customer or take the walk-in's phone where the store asks (only a *Required* store insists) → Hold or Charge → Tender (splitting across methods as needed) → receipt → New sale.
- *Age-restricted items:* scanning or picking an item that is age-restricted in the store's country stops before it reaches the sale and asks the cashier to check — the item, its category, the minimum age, and whether that age is the legal minimum or the store's own stricter policy, the country in words (*Legal minimum in the United Kingdom.*). **Refuse sale** keeps it out; **Checked — 18+** lets it in. The dialog cannot be dismissed by tapping away. One check covers the rest of the sale at that age, and is asked again for an item with a higher age, when the basket is cleared, or when a parked sale is resumed. **If the till can't find out** — the store has no country, the country has no rule for that category, or product-svc can't be reached — the item is refused with the reason, not waved through: a till that treats *couldn't tell* as *not restricted* sells alcohol to a child while every screen says it asked. Every decision is recorded (`POST /pos/age-checks`): **Refuse sale** asks why — under age, no ID, ID not accepted, buying for someone under age, other — and **Record refusal** writes it; **Checked — 18+** may note what was shown (passport, driving licence, PASS card, …). The decision stands whether or not the write succeeds — a network fault never turns a refusal into a sale — and the cashier is told to inform a manager if it could not be written. Managers read the register under **Admin → Age checks**: every check by store and period, the refusals and their reasons, the counts a licensing officer asks for first. **A date of birth, not an age:** where the rule has a birth-date cut-off (UK tobacco from 1 Jan 2027: nobody born on or after 1 Jan 2009, whatever their age), the dialog also says *And born before 1 Jan 2009*, whether that is the law or the store's own policy, the button reads **Checked — 18+, born before 1 Jan 2009**, and the refusal reasons gain *Born on or after the cut-off date*. A check for 18 earlier in the sale does not cover it — an age says nothing about the year someone was born — while one passed cut-off check covers the next pack. A cut-off the till cannot read keeps the item out. The customer's date of birth is never recorded, only the date the check was judged against.
- *Recalls:* the till keeps the list of open recalls, refreshed every five minutes, and checks every scanned or picked item against it before anything else. **Every pack recalled:** *Do not sell this item*, with the reference, the hazard and the customer notice, and one button — *Remove from sale*; there is no override. **Only some lots or dates recalled:** *Check the pack before selling*, listing exactly what to look for (*lot L-2291, best before 1 Oct 2026 to 31 Oct 2026*); *Affected — remove* is the prominent answer, *Not affected — sell* lets it in. When the list can't be refreshed for half an hour the till keeps selling against the list it has and shows *Recalls haven't refreshed for a while. Check items against the recall notices.* — blocking every sale because inventory-svc is down would close the shop.
- *Weighed items:* an item sold by weight, volume or length asks for the reading before it reaches the sale — the item, its price per unit, and *Enter the weight the approved scale shows. Do not estimate it.* The line price updates as the reading is typed (to the gram; a decimal comma is accepted). Where the product declares packaging weight the dialog says the scale deducts it, and the till subtracts nothing itself. In the basket a weighed line shows its reading (e.g. *0.375 kg*) and counts as one item; tapping it reads the scale again, and it has no ± buttons. The receipt prints *0.375 kg × £12.00/kg* (a thermal receipt keeps the currency code, which every printer code page can print). If the till can't tell how an item is sold it is kept out, not rung up as a single unit. Until this existed a line's quantity was a whole number and no loose item could be sold by weight at all. A parked weighed line resumes with its weight, though — as for every parked line — without its name or unit. Before it asks for a reading the till checks the store's instrument register: only a scale *certified for trade* is offered (one is picked silently; two or more ask *Which scale?*), and with none — or when the register cannot be read — the sale by weight is refused with the reason (Weights and Measures Act 1985 s.11). The line records which scale it was weighed on. A barcode printed by a certified labelling scale is read exactly by that scale's scheme — the item code, and the price or weight it encodes, check digit verified — and rings the item up already weighed on that scale, without the dialog; a label the scheme cannot read is looked up as an ordinary barcode. The counter scale's own reading is still keyed: a serial feed from the instrument needs certified hardware and an approval process, not an endpoint.
- *Reduced-price stickers (05.4):* a code in the shop's own range (thirteen digits, prefix 21) is asked about first — pricing-svc says which markdown it is, the product and the sticker's price — and the pack rings up at that price with a **REDUCED** badge and the price it was reduced from struck through (*£2.24 ~~was £2.99~~*) — only the was price pricing-svc says the law allows (03.12): the original price where the prior-price rule does not bind or exempts short-dated goods, otherwise the proven 30-day prior price. With none, the line carries a **MARKDOWN** badge and no was price. A sticker the server refuses — past its date, every pack sold, taken off — is kept out with that reason; a code shaped like a sticker that no live sticker carries is looked up as an ordinary barcode. The stickered packs and the same product at the list price are two lines, each with its own ±; nothing is weighed for a sticker, because the sticker prices the pack. The line carries its markdown to the order, so a promotion never applies on top of it and the sticker counts down; a parked sale keeps it, so a resumed sale still rings at the sticker.
- *Interrupted sale:* Hold → serve another customer → Resume (discarding or completing the interrupted cart).
- *Network drops mid-shift:* the sale completes at the till — the customer pays, the receipt prints with an offline reference — and the writes it owes the server are queued. The till keeps selling; the queue drains by itself when the line comes back. Sales that are still waiting survive a sign-out and an app restart, and clocking out warns how many are outstanding.
- *End of shift:* Close till → count cash → Z-report → Clock out.

**Notable UX/product features:** camera + hardware-scanner + manual-entry barcode input all funnel through the same lookup; "No sale" and cash drop/pay-in/pay-out give the till an auditable cash-management trail; receipts come out the way each till is set up to — the browser's print dialog, an ESC/POS thermal printer, or a file — see *Receipt printer* below; the HTML in a browser tab is the web default (a web-first, browser-print workflow rather than direct receipt-printer integration); Catalog-mode stores turn the POS into an order-taking terminal with no payment step at all; an offline store-and-forward queue means a dropped network stops the *syncing*, not the *selling*.

**Offline limits, stated plainly.** The queue covers completing a sale, not starting one: scanning still resolves the barcode and price against the server, so an offline till can finish a sale whose lines are already in the cart but cannot ring up a new item. A cached catalog is the separate, larger piece of work. Gift-card and store-credit tenders can only be *staged* while online (the balance lookup needs the server), so they never enter the queue from a cold-offline till — but a sale interrupted after they were staged carries them, and a balance that has since gone is surfaced as a parked sale rather than silently swallowed.

## 6. Storefront — public, customer-facing shop

**Persona:** end customers — guest or signed in — browsing and buying from a business's online shop. Reachable without login; has its own lightweight customer sign-in, separate from Admin/POS credentials.

**Home — Shop:** a store switcher at the top (only shown when the business has 2+ stores), an auto-rotating offers carousel driven by the business's live promotions that may be advertised — an item promotion only while every reduced price can be announced with its 30-day prior price (03.12) — falling back to generic content when none may; a product's promotional price shows *Summer · ~~was €12.00~~* beneath it only when that reduction may be announced, a search bar, category chips and an *In stock* chip, and the products. On a phone they are a list of rows (a thumbnail beside the name and price); wider, a grid of **product cards** (`ProductCard`): a flat card on its hairline with the large corner, the picture edge to edge above the name and — under it — the price in the page's ink (information, not an accent), its unit price, any reduction that may be announced, the stock and the amber add control, which turns into a − qty + stepper once the item is in the cart; the whole card opens the product, the add control inside it being its own button. As many columns as keep each card near 180–220px wide, never fewer than two, the cards widening and the room under the picture growing with enlarged text, so nothing is cut off. The first load shows grey placeholders shaped like the rows or cards to come (`Skeleton`), which hold still under reduced motion and read to a screen reader as *Loading products*, so the page does not jump from a spinner to a full shop. Offer banners are flat fills from the theme's container pairs, so they read as calmly in dark mode as in light (a spend-and-save names its threshold as money, *spend £25.00+*). A small self-promotional card for the platform itself is mixed into the product grid at a random position among the real products. A dark store in the switcher reads *Online hub · delivery only*.

Navigation: **Shop · Cart** (with a live item-count badge). A sticky cart bar (subtotal + item count + "View cart") floats above the content once something's in the basket — on every screen except the cart itself and, on a phone, a product page, where the page's own Add bar sits instead (the cart stays one tap away in the app bar and the bottom bar).

- **Preferences** — "Shopping for" (Myself/Family/Business, multi-select) and a notification preference (Order updates / Promotions / Both / None); shown once automatically right after first sign-in/registration if not yet answered, or reachable anytime.
- **Feedback** — category (Bug / Feature request / Compliment / Other), free-text description, optional 1–5 star rating; available any time.
- **Post-order survey** — a 1–5 emoji experience rating, a 1–10 "would you recommend us" slider, and an optional comment, meant to appear after checkout completes.
- **Store-unavailable notice** — if the business has been deactivated by the platform, the whole storefront shows a "currently unavailable" message instead of the shop.

**Flows:**
- *Guest browsing → first purchase:* land on Shop → (skippable preference prompt) → browse/search/filter → Product Detail → Add to cart → Cart → **sign in / create account (required here)** → choose Pickup/Delivery → pick a window where the store offers them → choose payment → Review → Place order/Pay → confirmation.
- *Returning customer:* Sign in → Preferences (first time) → shop → My Orders shows the full synced history.
- *Post-purchase engagement:* Order placed → (later) post-order survey; any time → Send feedback from the account menu.

**Notable UX/product features:** multi-store switching within one business's storefront; a native ad slot mixed into organic product listings; anonymous browsing with a low-friction sign-in gate placed at checkout (account creation is required to buy, but nowhere earlier); a duplicate-order guard; Catalog mode strips prices/payment consistently across product list, detail, cart, and order history; four lightweight, skippable customer-research surveys (preferences, feedback, post-order) built directly into the shopping experience.

## 7. Cross-cutting UX / product features

- **"Catalog mode"** (a per-store "show prices" toggle, set from Admin → Stores → Edit) turns off pricing everywhere that store's customers/staff touch it — storefront listings/detail/cart/orders show stock only ("Add," no price), the POS Tender screen skips payment collection entirely and becomes a "Place order" ticket, and receipts/orders show "Price on delivery"/"Price in store" instead of a total. This is a first-class, deliberately designed alternate business mode (e.g. for wholesale/quote-based or regulated-pricing retailers) — see [README §16](../README.md#16-catalog-mode--selling-without-showing-a-price).
- **Barcode scanning:** one shared full-screen camera scanner (with a flash/torch toggle) is reused in three places — Admin Products (tag a variant's barcode), Admin Inventory (resolve a variant when receiving stock), and POS Sale (ring up items) — always with manual text entry as a fallback, since desktop browsers have no camera-scanning path.
- **Dark mode:** full light/dark theming that follows the OS setting; a warm ivory/charcoal/amber/forest-green palette with dedicated, colour-blind-safe success/warning colors (always paired with text or an icon, never colour alone).
- **Localization:** 8 languages ship at the framework level — English, Polish, Romanian, Punjabi, Urdu, Bengali, Gujarati, Arabic. **No country is assumed:** a device in English keeps its own English region where intl has one (en-IN, en-US, en-AU, en-ZA, en-IE, en-CA, en-NZ, en-SG, en-GB) and gets plain English otherwise, so dates and numbers follow the reader rather than the UK; a device in a language the app does not ship falls back to plain English. Urdu and Arabic auto-mirror the whole layout right-to-left. In practice, only the sign-in pages' strings are translated today — Login/Register, *Forgot password?* and the request and reset pages (all eight languages), the password rule and iam-svc's refusals of a password, *Sign in with your business* and every single-sign-on refusal — and the rest of the interface is still English, with translation coverage layered on top of already-complete RTL/date/number framework support. Money, counts and dates follow the language the app runs in: `main.dart` sets intl's default locale from the resolved app locale before anything is formatted, and every number and date on screen goes through `AppFormat` — `test/core/format_guard_test.dart` fails a `NumberFormat` or `DateFormat` built anywhere else, because one made without a locale pins the whole app to the system's `en_US`.
- **Multi-currency:** every money-creating flow (onboarding, stores, price lists, promotions, gift cards, purchase orders/suppliers, layaways) offers the same 5 currencies (INR/USD/GBP/SGD/AED) tied to the 5 supported countries. **Exchange rates (03.x):** Admin → Pricing carries an **Exchange rates** card (management): the home currency, each rate in force with its day and reason, and **Set a rate** (currency, home units per unit, from a day, reason — refusals shown by name); the storefront app bar gets a **currency picker** listing the shop's own currency and those it keeps a rate for, each listed as *USD, shown at the shop's rate* or *GBP (you pay in GBP)*, and every price, card and basket total then shows the converted figure as money marked ≈ (*≈ US$15.00*) beside the one charged, the cart adding *≈ US$15.00 at the shop's rate; you pay in GBP*.
- **Receipts:** generated as printable HTML opened in a new browser tab that auto-invokes the browser's print dialog (a web-first design), money on it as the shopper reads it (*£12.00*) and the sale's date and time in the till's own time (*25 Oct 2026 14:05*) — a thermal (ESC/POS) receipt keeps currency codes, which every printer code page can print; the POS "email receipt" action records the audit row **and** delivers a plain-text receipt via notification-svc (SMTP when `storeql.notification.channel=email`, otherwise the APP log channel). Under a fiscal regime (18.5) the till reads the whole stamp with the number and prints it: a German receipt carries the module's serial, transaction number, signature counter, start and end, the signature and the QR payload (KassenSichV §6), or *Sicherheitseinrichtung ausgefallen* with the reason when the module was down; a Portuguese receipt carries the ATCUD, the four characters of the signature and *Processado por programa certificado n.º NNNN/AT*.
- **Auditable "soft" cash/inventory actions:** POS "No sale" and cash drop/pay-in/pay-out are explicit, logged, non-sale operations distinct from a Charge — a loss-prevention/reconciliation feature.
- **Reliability touches:** temp passwords for newly-provisioned staff accounts are shown exactly once (masked by default, explicit reveal + copy, with a "clear your clipboard" reminder); a few sensitive create actions (special orders, POS payment collection) are safe to retry without double effect; tenant deactivation is enforced end-to-end (the storefront shows a "closed" notice rather than a raw error).
- **Reports:** every report with rows offers client-side CSV export; period reports carry a date bar and grouped reports carry grouping chips. One format trap is handled in the client rather than left to the caller: the date pickers speak `yyyy-MM-dd`, but the inventory and pricing report endpoints parse `from`/`to` with `Instant.parse` and reject a bare date, so the client widens a day to `T00:00:00Z`/`T23:59:59Z` — `to` covering the *end* of its day, or a report run "to today" would silently exclude today.

### 7.1 Material 3 conventions

- **Color:** `AppTheme.lightScheme`/`.darkScheme` (`core/theme.dart`) write out **every** Material 3 role explicitly (primary…tertiary + containers, error, the five surface-container tones, inverse roles) — nothing is algorithm-derived, so the design system mirrors the file one-to-one. Light sits on warm off-white `#FAF8F3`, dark on warm charcoal `#161513` (never pure white/black); every text role is ≥4.5:1 on every surface container in both themes. Cards use `surfaceContainerLowest` (light) / `surfaceContainerLow` (dark) with an `outlineVariant` hairline, no shadow; the app bar is surface-coloured (POS overrides it). Don't hand-add a one-off `Color(0x...)`; read `Theme.of(context).colorScheme`, `context.status` (success/warning/info, each with a container pair) or `context.channelAccent`, and use `AppRadius` / `AppSpacing` instead of magic numbers.
- **Channel accents (POS):** the POS terminal's amber accent is a `ChannelAccent` `ThemeExtension` (soft amber in light, deep amber container in dark) applied via `AppTheme.applyPosAccent()`, scoped to the POS subtree by `PosShell`'s `Theme(...)` wrapper — not a raw `Color` constant threaded through widgets. Read it with `context.channelAccent.{color,onColor}` so the background and its contrasting foreground always travel together.
- **Platforms:** one look everywhere, native manners from `AppTheme` (keyed on `defaultTargetPlatform`, which on the web is the browser's OS): centred app-bar titles on iOS/macOS; compact density, 40dp targets, a slim scrollbar and a 440dp snackbar in desktop browsers; 48dp targets on touch. Lay out by width, not platform: `context.windowClass` / `AppBreakpoints` (600 / 800 rail / 840 / 1200), `context.pagePadding`, and `ContentBounds` (1200) / `ContentBounds.form` (640) so web pages don't stretch. The rail starts labelled from 1200. Use `.adaptive` constructors for platform-convention controls (`SwitchListTile.adaptive`), `SafeArea` for bottom-pinned bars, `EdgeInsetsDirectional` for RTL. Launch screens (Android `values`/`values-night`, iOS `LaunchBackground` colour set, web splash + `theme-color`) match `surface` in light and dark.
- **Source of truth:** the theme mirrors the StoreQL **Claude design system** (artifact `2507697b-5dab-4a2e-8ac9-2a27b0d8b4fb`: `project/README.md` is the brand book, `project/tokens.json` the tokens, one README per component). Change a token there first, then here; `test/core/theme_contrast_test.dart` holds both to 4.5:1.
- **States:** `EmptyState` (an outlined icon — an inbox by default — on a 72px `surfaceContainerHigh` disc, a title that names the situation rather than the mechanism — *No purchase orders yet*, *No customers match “Leeds”*, never *0 results* — one sentence, at most one action) for anything empty — never a bare "No data"; `ErrorView` (the icon on an `errorContainer` disc, the message in `onSurface`, a tonal *Retry*) for a failure; `Skeleton` for a first load whose layout is known — grey `SkeletonBlock`s and `SkeletonLine`s (as tall as the text they stand for, at the shopper's text size) laid out like the content, one soft highlight sweeping across them all, holding still when the device asks for less motion, and read to a screen reader as one label (*Loading products*), never as the blocks; it gives way to the content or to an `ErrorView` (the storefront's shop and My orders use it); `LoadingView` for a wait with no known shape. A status shows as a `StatusBadge` — words on a status container, `AppRadius.badge` corners (see *Words, not codes* in §7.2).
- **Elevation:** StoreQL is flat — cards sit on an `outlineVariant` hairline with no shadow. `AppShadow` (`core/theme.dart`) holds the design system's three shadows for something that floats, never a card: `level1` an elevated button, `level2` a FAB or a bar that content scrolls under (`BottomActionBar(raised: true)`, the storefront's checkout bar), `level3` snack bars and menus.
- **Radii:** `AppRadius` (`core/theme.dart`) is the only source of corner radii — the scale `xs` 4 · `sm` 8 · `md` 12 · `lg` 16 · `xl` 24 · `full` (stadium), and the shapes built on it: `badge` (4), `chip` (8), `input` (12), `card` (16), `sheet` (24, top corners) and `pill` (stadium). Features never write `BorderRadius.circular(<number>)`; the theme's component themes already round cards, fields, chips, dialogs and sheets, so most widgets need no radius at all.
- **Status badges:** a badge for a state is a `StatusBadge`, which sets its words in `on*Container` on `context.status.*Container` (success, warning, info; error on `errorContainer`, neutral on `surfaceContainerHigh`, accent on `primaryContainer`), never a status colour with an alpha — the containers are tuned to 4.5:1 in both brightnesses and an alpha tint is not.
- **Hard-coded colours:** none in features or shared widgets, with two deliberate exceptions — the camera viewfinder (`barcode_scanner_sheet`: black behind a live camera) and the QR painter (`mfa_widgets`: scanners need black on white). Storefront offer banners and the sponsored tile draw a flat scheme fill with its own ink (`_toneColors` in `product_list_screen`: the primary, secondary and tertiary `*Container` / `on*Container` pairs; the sponsored tile on `tertiaryContainer`, its badge on `secondaryContainer`), and product placeholders take `ProductThumb.tones`. Both are held to 4.5:1 by `test/core/theme_contrast_test.dart`, which also measures every scheme role, surface container, outline, status colour and the POS accent in both brightnesses.
- **Navigation:** `AdaptiveNavShell`'s `compactStyle` picks the phone-width pattern — `CompactNavStyle.drawer` (default, for 6+ destinations) or `CompactNavStyle.bottomBar` (up to 5). Don't add a 6th+ destination to a shell that uses `bottomBar` without reconsidering the pattern. A shell with more destinations than a rail holds (about seven) gives each one a `section`: Admin's 31 pages are grouped under *Sell*, *Products & stock*, *Safety & compliance*, *Money*, *Business* and *Data & security*, shown as a labelled panel beside the content from 1200px (the app-bar icon hides it for more room and brings it back) and as a drawer below it, with a *Find a page* box (Ctrl+K / ⌘K) and the selected page always scrolled into view. Every destination has its own icon.
- **Icons:** the nav-destination convention is `_outlined` for unselected, filled (no suffix) for selected (see any `AdaptiveNavDestination`) — follow that pairing for new selectable icons. Elsewhere, prefer `_outlined` as the default icon style; the existing mix of filled/outlined glyphs on non-selectable icons predates this note and hasn't been swept, so match the surrounding screen rather than the nearest icon.
- **Banners:** use `MaterialBanner` (see `_ExpiringBanner` in `inventory_screen.dart` or `_SignInBanner` in `storefront/orders_screen.dart`) for a persistent, dismissible, 1–2-action message — not a hand-rolled `Container`/`Material` block.
- **Deferred (needs a product/data decision, not just a widget swap):** a Time picker for store business hours — `businessHours` is currently a raw `String` with no structured per-day open/close shape, so there's no data model for a picker to write into yet. A `RangeSlider` price filter on the storefront catalog — product-svc/pricing-svc don't currently accept a min/max price query param, so the control would have nothing to filter against.

### 7.2 Adaptive standard (phones, tablets, desktop)

The same screen runs on an Android phone, an iPhone, an iPad at the counter and a desktop browser. These rules keep it right on all of them; the building blocks named here are in `lib/shared/widgets/` and `lib/core/spacing.dart`.

- **Test every screen at four sizes:** phone 390×844 (and 360 wide), tablet portrait 820×1180, tablet landscape 1180×820, desktop 1440×900 — plus 200% text and a right-to-left language. The back office opens its menu as a drawer below 1200px, so a back-office page gets the full width on a tablet; POS and storefront lose 80px to the rail from 800px.
- **Decide layout from the width you have, not the device.** `LayoutBuilder` → `AppBreakpoints.classOf(constraints.maxWidth)` gives compact (< 600), medium (600–839), expanded (840–1199) or large (≥ 1200). Use these four classes, not ad-hoc numbers (700, 880…). Use `context.windowClass` only for app chrome. Never branch on `Platform.isIOS` for layout.
- **Input follows the platform, not the width:** `pointerFirst` (`core/input_mode.dart`) is true on desktop OSes, including a desktop browser. Mouse-and-keyboard gets shortcuts (Ctrl+K / ⌘K find a page, F2 barcode on the till), hover states and visible scrollbars; touch gets pull-to-refresh (`RefreshIndicator.adaptive`), bottom sheets and 48dp targets. Keep a refresh button for mouse users; every hover has a tap equivalent.
- **Page frame:** `PageHeader` for the title and the page's own actions (one row from 600px, actions wrap under the title on phones and at large text); inset the content below by the same `context.pageGutter` (16 / 24). `PageHeader(stackBelow:)` moves the actions under the title sooner where a long subtitle would otherwise break mid-word beside them (Chargebacks and Card settlements ask for 600). Cap widths on desktop: `ContentBounds` (1200) for pages, `ContentBounds.form` (640) for forms, and `ContentBounds.reading` for running text — `AppBreakpoints.readingMeasure` (552px, about 80 characters of body text a line, WCAG 1.4.8) plus the page gutters — which the accessibility statement and the storefront's privacy page use.
- **App bars:** `AdaptiveActions` — every command from 600px, one or two on a phone and the rest in ⋮. Never let actions push the title out.
- **Lists and tables:** on phones, a row's text keeps at least ~60% of the width — trailing steppers, prices and badges move to a line under the text. Wide tables sit in `ScrollableTable` so every column and row is reachable at tablet widths. Money in a `ListTile` trailing slot sets its own style (`titleSmall`), or it inherits 11px `labelSmall`. A list under a FAB pads its end by `AppSpacing.fabClearance`.
- **Filters:** `AdaptiveFilters` — inline from 600px, behind one *Filters · n* button on phones so the list starts on the first screen (the Audit trail); *Clear filters* moves under the button rather than overflowing a narrow row.
- **Primary action on phones:** `BottomActionBar` pinned under the scroll (Checkout, Charge, Complete sale, Add), clear of the home indicator; from 600px it sits at the end of the content. `raised: true` lifts it on `shadow-2` instead of the hairline, for a bar that content scrolls under — the storefront's `OrderSummaryBar`. In use on the POS Tender screen, the storefront product page and the cart.
- **Panels:** `showAdaptiveSheet` — a bottom sheet on phones, a centred dialog wider.
- **Words, not codes:** statuses through `StatusBadge` with words (`orderStatusLabel`, `channelLabel`, `materialStatusLabel`, a screen's own map, `humanizeCode` as the fallback) and a tone (info = waiting, warning = partial, success = done, error = failed, neutral = closed). Money always `AppFormat.money` — `maxDecimals: 4` for a price set finer than the currency's minor unit (*£0.035*), which otherwise reads as plain money — counts and quantities `AppFormat.count` (grouped, a ledger's trailing zeros dropped, a part unit keeping its fraction), dates `AppFormat.date` / `dateOf` / `dateTime`, a day read at a glance (a delivery or collection window, the slot picker's days) `AppFormat.weekdayDate` — the weekday and date in the reader's own order, *Sun 27 Sept* or *Sun, Sep 27*, and a window worded once for every screen by `slotWindowLabel` / `slotWhen` (`shared/util/slot_label.dart`) — a time of day `AppFormat.time`; a country by `countryName` / `countryInSentence` (*in the United Kingdom*, `core/reference/iso_reference.dart`). People and things by name: a member of staff by login email (`staffDisplayName` over `StaffNameCache` in `admin/providers/staff_names.dart`, which asks iam-svc's `GET /auth/admin/staff-users?ids=` once per person), a product by `variantDisplayName` (`admin/providers/admin_providers.dart`) or `VariantNames` / `VariantLine` (`admin/widgets/variant_names.dart`), a store from the store list — `shortRef(id)` (the last 8 characters) only while no name is known.
- **Empty and error states:** `EmptyState` names the situation and offers the next step; errors go through `friendlyError`, never a raw exception.
- **Large text and RTL:** never fix the height of a box that holds text (badges, chips, rows grow); use `EdgeInsetsDirectional` and `start`/`end`.
