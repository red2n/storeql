## Problem

A picker opens an online order and one line is not on the shelf. Today the platform's only answers are to leave the line waiting for the next pick — an order that never completes, a parcel that never leaves — or to hand over the rest and let the shopper discover at the door that a third of what they paid for is missing, with the money for it still taken. Nothing lets the store put a like-for-like product in the bag, nothing refunds what could not be supplied, and the shopper is never asked whether they would accept a swap or told when one was made.

## Outcome

At checkout the shopper says whether substitutions are welcome. When a picker finds a line short, they either close the line short — the unfilled quantity is taken off the order and the money for it goes back — or, where the shopper allowed it, put a substitute in the bag: the platform suggests the products the business declared as stand-ins that are on the shelf, prices the substitute at the store and **never charges more than the original**, refunds any difference, and tells the shopper what was swapped and what they pay. Either way the order completes, is handed over, and reads truthfully: what was supplied, what was swapped, what was refunded. A substitute the shopper does not want is refused at handover and refunded as a return.

## Who and where

- **Personas** ([PRD §2](../PRD.md)): the picker and the store staff at a shop or dark store; the online shopper, who chooses and is told; the manager, who declares substitutes in the catalogue and reads what was swapped or short.
- **Channels:** ONLINE orders for PICKUP or DELIVERY, in CONFIRMED or PARTIALLY_FULFILLED; the back office (Fulfilment screen) and the storefront (the checkout choice, the messages). A till sale is never short or substituted: the goods are in the customer's hand.
- **Scope:** per store; a line is closed or substituted at the order's store by staff assigned there. Substitutes are declared per tenant in product-svc.
- **Roles that can write:** any member of staff assigned to the order's store (CASHIER, STOREKEEPER, MANAGER, OWNER), as the handover is — the picker is who finds the gap; the shopper's `allowSubstitutions` at checkout; a customer never closes or substitutes a line.
- **Sandbox tenant:** behaves the same.

## Scope

- **In:**
  - **The shopper's choice.** `allowSubstitutions` on the order at checkout (`POST /orders`), default per the open question; the storefront's checkout carries a switch; the order and its summaries say it.
  - **Close a line short.** `POST /orders/{id}/lines/{variantId}/short {qty?, reason?}` on a CONFIRMED or PARTIALLY_FULFILLED online order: the quantity (default: all still outstanding) is recorded as short on the line (`short_qty`), the line's charge and VAT reduced pro rata, the order's totals recomputed, and the order becomes FULFILLED when every line is either picked or closed — so it can be handed over. Publishes `OrderLineShortClosed` (the buyer, the variant, the quantity, the refund due, the order's new total): payment-svc refunds the shopper's tender for the unfilled amount (nothing when nothing was captured — a pay-later order simply owes less at handover), inventory-svc releases the hold for that quantity and shortens the waiting line, notification-svc tells the shopper.
  - **Substitute a line.** `POST /orders/{id}/lines/{variantId}/substitute {substituteVariantId, qty?}` on the same orders, only when the order allows substitutions: a new line for the substitute — priced at the order's store by pricing-svc, **charged at no more than the original line's unit price (gross)**, its VAT at the substitute's own rate within that price — marked as substituting the original, the original closed short for that quantity, the substitute picked at once (it is in the picker's hand: `OrderFulfilled` for the new line deducts the stock and records the revenue), totals recomputed, the difference refunded. Publishes `OrderLineSubstituted` (from, to, quantity, charged, refund due) beside the `OrderFulfilled`. `GET /orders/{id}/lines/{variantId}/substitutes` suggests stand-ins: the business's `SUBSTITUTE` relationships for the variant (product-svc), each with what the store has available (inventory-svc) and its name and SKU.
  - **The shopper told:** `ORDER_LINE_SUBSTITUTED` (what was swapped for what, the quantity, what they pay, the refund if any, and that they may refuse it at handover) and `ORDER_LINE_SHORT` (what could not be supplied and what was refunded); both catalogued, email and push. Both events in the webhook catalogue.
  - **A refused substitute** is a return at handover, as today (`POST /orders/{id}/returns` on the substitute line): refunded, restocked.
  - **The Fulfilment screen** gains **Outstanding lines**: the store's confirmed and part-picked orders with what each line still owes, whether the shopper allows substitutions, and per line **Substitute** (the suggestions with availability, or any variant by id, the quantity) and **Short-close** (quantity, reason). The order's answer carries each line's `shortQty`, `outstandingQty` and, for a substitute, `substitutesVariantId`; the order's `allowSubstitutions`.
  - **Refund kind:** payment-svc's `PaymentRefunded` gains `kind` (`SALE_REVERSAL` for a return or cancellation, `ORDER_ADJUSTMENT` for a short close or substitution) so order-svc's `applyRefundOnce` records the money but does not move an adjusted order to PARTIALLY_REFUNDED — the goods are still to be handed over.
- **Out, on purpose:**
  - **Automatic substitution by the platform** (a rule picks the stand-in with nobody looking): the picker decides, from the business's declared substitutes; the platform suggests and prices.
  - **Per-line preferences** ("substitute this but not that") and a shopper approving or refusing a swap in the app before handover: the choice is per order at checkout, and refusal is at handover. An in-app "refuse before it ships" is a later refinement.
  - **Re-quoting the basket** after a short close or substitution: a whole-basket promotion or coupon the order carried stays as it was; the shopper is never charged more than they paid, and a promotion they might no longer qualify for is the business's small loss, not a surprise charge.
  - **Charging more for a dearer substitute**, or re-authorising a card: never; the price is capped at the original.
  - **Substitutions at the till, or on a wave's pick list**: the wave leaves the shortfall waiting; the substitution is made on the outstanding line afterwards, from the Fulfilment screen.
  - **Weighed lines** substituted by weight, and cross-category suggestions by any rule other than the declared relationships (same category, price band, machine learning): later.
  - **Showing substitutions in the shopper's order history**: the storefront has no order detail screen; the message is the shopper's record for now.

## Data and flow

- **Owned by** order-svc: `orders.allow_substitutions` (the checkout's choice); `order_items.short_qty` (never handed over; `outstanding = qty − fulfilled_qty − short_qty`; a line's `line_total` and `vat_amount` reduced pro rata to what stands) and `order_items.substitutes_item_id` (the line a substitute line replaces); `order_line_adjustments` (append-only: kind SHORT_CLOSED | SUBSTITUTED, the original line and variant, the quantity, the substitute line and variant, the amount charged, the refund due, the reason, who, when); the order's `subtotal`, `tax_amount`, `total` recomputed (as `/price` recomputes them) and a status-history line per adjustment; `orders.status` moves to FULFILLED when every line is picked or closed. Container deposits (09.16) on a short-closed drink line are reduced pro rata with it.
- **Needs from other services:** product-svc `GET /admin/products/variants/{id}/relationships` (SUBSTITUTE) and `/variants/resolve` (names, SKUs) — REST, as staff; inventory-svc `GET /admin/inventory/network/stock?variants=` (what the store has) — REST; pricing-svc `quoteBasket` for the substitute at the order's store — REST, as today's checkout does; nothing joined.
- **Events published:** `OrderLineShortClosed {eventId, occurredAt, tenantId, orderId, storeId, customerId, loginId, variantId, qty, refundAmount, currency, orderTotal}`; `OrderLineSubstituted {…, fromVariantId, toVariantId, qty, chargedAmount, refundAmount, currency, orderTotal}`; `OrderFulfilled` for the substitute line (its `outstanding` naming the original variant's new figure, so the waiting list drops it). Consumers: inventory-svc (both: release the hold for the quantity, shorten the waiting line — a substituted original is released and the substitute is deducted by the `OrderFulfilled`), payment-svc (both: refund `refundAmount` against the order's tenders, `kind: ORDER_ADJUSTMENT`, once per event), notification-svc (both: the two messages), webhooks. `PaymentRefunded` gains `kind`.
- **Retryable writes** (Idempotency-Key): `POST …/short` and `POST …/substitute` take one — a retried press must not close or substitute twice; the key is looked up first and the same answer returned.
- **New error codes:** `ORDER_LINE_NOT_ADJUSTABLE` 409 (not a CONFIRMED or PARTIALLY_FULFILLED online order — a till sale, a cancelled or handed-over order); `ORDER_LINE_UNKNOWN` 400 (no such variant on the order); `ORDER_LINE_QTY_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING` 409 (more than the line still owes); `ORDER_SUBSTITUTION_NOT_ALLOWED` 409 (the shopper said no); `ORDER_SUBSTITUTE_SAME_VARIANT` 400; `ORDER_SUBSTITUTE_NOT_SELLABLE` 409 (pricing-svc has no price for it at the store, or inventory-svc has none of it there); `ORDER_PRICING_UNAVAILABLE` 503 as at checkout; `403 STORE_ACCESS_DENIED`; `404 ORDER_NOT_FOUND` for another tenant's order, whatever the caller's role or store scope; `400 VALIDATION_FAILED` (a quantity below 0.001, a reason over 200 characters).

## Money, time and limits

- **Currency:** the order's; the substitute is priced in it at the order's store; every amount `BigDecimal` to the currency's minor units, the last unit of a pro-rata share taking the rounding.
- **Ledger postings:** none new here — a refund posts as a refund does; the substitute's revenue rides `OrderFulfilled` as any line's; purchase-svc's sales posting reads `OrderConfirmed` and is untouched (a later reconciliation of adjusted orders is its own row).
- **Dates:** the adjustment's instant, UTC.
- **Plan limits:** none.

## Constraints

Database-per-service: substitutes are asked of product-svc, stock of inventory-svc, prices of pricing-svc, and the refund is payment-svc's from an event. Event consumers idempotent: each event carries an `eventId`; payment-svc refunds once per event (`refundOrderOnce`), inventory-svc releases once (the hold quantity cannot go below zero; a redelivery finds nothing to release), notification-svc sends once. Append-only stays append-only: `order_line_adjustments`, `order_status_history`, payments and refunds; the order row's totals are recomputed as `/price` already does. Money is never taken twice and never more than the original: the substitute's gross unit price is `min(quoted, original)`; the refund is the original gross for the quantity less the substitute's charged gross, never negative. The shopper's `allowSubstitutions` is read from the order, never from the request that substitutes. Store scope on every write; the picked substitute is deducted through the same `OrderFulfilled` path every deduction takes, so a wave's credits and the once-only handover keep working.

## Open questions

- [x] **Are substitutions allowed by default?** Recommended: yes — the checkout's switch is on unless the shopper turns it off (the grocery norm; most shoppers would rather have a like-for-like than a gap), and the order records the choice. The alternative is off by default, which makes every substitution wait on a shopper who did not read the switch. → **on by default; the order records the choice** (the user, 2026-09-25)
- [x] **What does the shopper pay for a substitute?** Recommended: the lower of the substitute's price at the store and the original line's price — never more than they paid, and the difference refunded when the stand-in is cheaper. The alternative charges the original price whatever the substitute costs, which keeps the refund away but has the shopper overpay for a cheaper swap. → **the lower of the two; the difference refunded** (the user, 2026-09-25)
- [x] **Where may a picker choose a substitute from?** Recommended: the business's declared substitutes (product-svc's `SUBSTITUTE` relationships) are suggested first, with what the store has of each, but the picker may name any variant the store sells and has — the shelf is the truth on the day. The alternative confines the picker to the declared list, which leaves a line short whenever the catalogue is thin. → **declared substitutes suggested first; any variant the store sells and has allowed** (the user, 2026-09-25)
- [x] **How does a shopper refuse a substitute?** Recommended: at handover, as a return — the counter or the driver takes it back and the existing return refunds and restocks it; the message says so. The alternative is an in-app approval before the parcel leaves, which holds the order until the shopper answers. → **at handover, as a return** (the user, 2026-09-25)

## Decisions

- **The substitute's own draw rides `OrderFulfilled`, not the new events.** A substitute line is inserted already picked (`fulfilled_qty = qty`) and the adjustment's transaction writes an `OrderFulfilled` naming only that line, with `outstanding` for every variant. So inventory-svc deducts it through the one path every deduction takes (holds, wave credits, per-line dedupe, revenue), and `OrderLineSubstituted` only has to give the original's hold back. A second deduction path would have had to reproduce all of that.
- **Adjustment refunds keep the order's status.** `PaymentRefunded.kind = ORDER_ADJUSTMENT` makes `applyRefundOnce` record the money and write a history line without moving to PARTIALLY_REFUNDED or REFUNDED: the goods are still to be handed over, and the total was already lowered by as much. A return's or a cancellation's refund carries no kind and moves the status as before. Any consumer reading `PaymentRefunded` without the field sees what it always did.
- **The cap is on the gross unit price, and VAT is the substitute's own.** The shopper never pays more than the original's gross per unit; within a capped price the substitute's net and VAT are recomputed at its own rate (`SubstitutePrice.charge`), so the fiscal file still lists the sale by the rate that applies to what was actually sold. Without a rate, the quote's own proportion holds; without VAT at all, the charge is all net.
- **The repository judges an empty line; the service does not pre-empt it.** `outstandingOf` returns what the line owes, possibly zero, and `adjustLine` answers `ORDER_LINE_NOT_ADJUSTABLE` for a picked or till order before it looks at the quantity, so a picked order is told it has no line to close rather than that zero exceeds zero.
- **The shelf refuses only when it can be read.** `ORDER_SUBSTITUTE_NOT_SELLABLE` for a substitute the store has less of than the quantity; an unreadable inventory-svc refuses nothing (the picker has the thing in hand), and a supplier-shipped product is held nowhere and passes — the same fail-open the router uses.
- **A hold shrinks rather than being released and re-placed.** `InventoryRepository.lineClosedOnce` reduces the order's HELD reservation on the variant by the closed quantity (a RELEASE movement for that much; released in full, with `StockReleased`, when nothing is left), and shortens the awaiting line on the same transaction, marked once per event. The awaiting-line helpers became package-private on `WaveRepository` for it, rather than a second copy.
- **The history carries the picker's reason.** "short: 1 × … , refund 10.00 (last one bruised)" — the adjustment row holds it too, but the history is what the Orders screen shows.
- **The stand-ins are read from product-svc as the service, not as the picker.** `GET /admin/products/variants/{id}/relationships` is a management read in product-svc (every `/admin/products/**` GET but `resolve` is), and a picker is a cashier or storekeeper; order-svc has already checked them against the order's store, so `ProductClient.substitutes` sends `MANAGER` as `StockClient` sends `STOREKEEPER` to inventory-svc, forwarding the picker's login for the record. Found by the k6 flow on the real stack: the integration stub had not enforced product-svc's roles.
- **A line is closed or substituted from the waiting list, and the k6 flow waits for it.** inventory-svc's waiting line is a projection of `OrderConfirmed` on its own consumer group; a close or substitution that lands before the projection has written the line reduces nothing, and the line then appears with its full quantity (seen once on the real stack: a substitution three seconds after confirmation). The picker works from that list, so the flow — like ship-from-store's — asserts the order waits at the shop before touching a line; a substitution made before the list knows the order is a rare, self-correcting over-count of one line on one screen, and the holds and the money are right regardless.
- **With server-side pricing off, the request's `unitPrice` prices the substitute and is still capped.** The dev rig and the integration tests run that way; the cap and the refund are then exercised end to end without pricing-svc, and the pricing-enforced path (quote, VAT, `ORDER_SUBSTITUTE_NOT_SELLABLE` for no price, `ORDER_PRICING_UNAVAILABLE`) is covered by `OrderServiceSubstituteTest` and the k6 flow against the real stack.
