## Problem

The inbox is built and proven: a supplier's UBL, CII, Factur-X or (from Poland's KSeF) FA(3) invoice is read, checked, matched to a supplier, an order and its lines, and captured through the three-way match; a network can push one in, and Poland's can be fetched. What the review still calls partial is not the reading. It is that the platform cannot yet say, for a business in a country where receiving is mandatory, that it **meets the duty**, and three real gaps sit behind that:

- **The duty is not data.** The obligations sheet lists `E_INVOICING_RECEIVE` for Germany and France and the KSeF duty for Poland, but nothing in the inbox reads them: a mandated business gets no statement of *what it must be able to do, from when, and whether it can*, and a PDF from a domestic supplier after the mandate date looks like any other document.
- **There is no provider seam on the receiving side.** A network delivers over one route guarded by one key for the whole deployment; a business has no connection of its own, nothing proves the delivery came from the provider that business chose, there is no way to *pull* from a provider that does not push, and there is nothing to send an answer back through.
- **Nothing goes back to the sender, and nothing is proved kept.** A refusal, an approval or a payment is not told to the network (France's platform requires a refusal to travel as a status; Peppol carries an invoice response), and the archive of what was received, in the form it arrived, has no guard, no export and no check. Worst, a network **delivery** is refused when the plan's document allowance is full, which for a mandated business means a legal invoice is turned away at the door.

## Outcome

- **A business in a country that mandates receiving sees exactly what is asked of it,** from which date, with the law's citation, and whether it can do it today: a connection chosen, a route that works, a receiving address registered, an archive that is guarded. The answer comes from the obligations data, never from a list in code.
- **A business connects its own provider,** the simulated network, an access point, France's platform or Poland's KSeF, each with its own sealed credential, and a delivery is accepted only from the connection that owns it. A provider that does not push is fetched from.
- **A document delivered over a network is never turned away for want of allowance:** it is kept, and the owner is told the pool is full.
- **A refusal, an approval and a payment go back to the sender** in the network's own words, where the network offers it, and always where the law requires it. The answer is sent once, retried when the network could not be reached, and kept.
- **The archive is provable:** every received document is kept in the form it arrived for at least the period the business's country sets, cannot be edited or deleted inside it, and can be exported for an inspector with a manifest whose hashes are checked.
- **What only a provider contract can finish is named** (below), and everything up to that line is built and tested against stubs.

## Who and where

- **Personas** ([PRD §2](../PRD.md)): the **owner** who chooses the provider and sees the duty, the **finance user** and **buyer** who process the inbox, the **inspector** (an accountant or tax officer the owner hands an export to; they have no login).
- **Channels:** back-office, the network itself (a delivery), and the platform's own simulated network.
- **Scope:** per business (the connection and the archive); a document belongs to the business its network connection or its buyer identity names. Not per store.
- **Roles that can write:** connections and their credentials, and the response settings: OWNER (a business-wide setting: a manager held to stores is refused `403 BUSINESS_WIDE_ONLY`; a business-wide MANAGER may read). Fetching and processing the inbox stays OWNER/MANAGER as today. Refusing, approving and paying stay with the roles that already decide those; the answer back is a consequence, not a separate act. Archive export and verify: OWNER and MANAGER holding the existing statutory-return permission, or `finance.journal`.
- **Sandbox tenant:** connects only the SIMULATED provider; nothing leaves the platform (`TenantProfiles.Profile.sandbox()`).

## Scope

- **In** (slices, in build order):
  1. **The duty as data, and readiness against it (tenant-svc data, purchase-svc read).** `legal_obligations` already carries `E_INVOICING_RECEIVE` (DE from 1 Jan 2025, FR from 1 Sep 2026) and `E_INVOICING_KSEF` (PL). This slice adds rows, **each with its own citation and effective date, in tenant-svc's migrations and never in code:** Belgium and any other country the Review names as it comes into force; `E_INVOICE_STRUCTURED_ONLY` (from the date a country stops accepting a non-structured invoice as an invoice, for the country's domestic B2B suppliers, with its transitional dates: the German issuing rule is 1 Jan 2027, so before then a PDF from a supplier is still an ordinary document); `E_INVOICE_ARCHIVE` (the archive must keep the structured original, with a citation such as Umsatzsteuergesetz §14b for Germany); and `E_INVOICE_REFUSAL_STATUS` (France: a refusal is transmitted as a lifecycle status). `Jurisdictions.Obligation` already exposes limit and qualifier columns (0.4); a row that binds only some businesses says so in its `qualifier` (a size band, for France's staged dates) and **a business declares its band in its own settings** (`einvoice_inbox_settings.size_band`, unset = the earliest date, the safe reading), never inferred. The citations are the compliance owner's to verify before a row goes live and are part of the data. `GET /admin/e-invoices/inbox/duty` answers, for the business's country today: each obligation in force or coming, its date and citation, and a checklist of what the business has (a connection chosen, its last delivery or fetch and its age, a receiving address on the business's identity, the archive guard on, the answer-back working where required), each item OK, MISSING or NOT_APPLICABLE. It is read-only and never fetches. **A document from a domestic supplier that is not structured**, arriving when `E_INVOICE_STRUCTURED_ONLY` is in force for the business's country, carries the warning `NOT_STRUCTURED_UNDER_MANDATE`: it is **kept and processed as any document, never refused**, because the goods are here and the liability is real, and the warning is shown so the business can ask the supplier for a proper one. The country of the seller is the one the document itself states.
  2. **A connection of its own, and the receiving provider seam (purchase-svc).** An `InboundNetwork` interface, mirroring order-svc's `EInvoiceTransport` and following the driver kit (`docs/DRIVERS.md`: catalogue row, `ck_einvoice_connection_provider`, sealed credentials under `storeql.einvoice.secrets-key` from `.env`, `service/` HTTP-free, an ArchUnit rule): `name()`, `networks()`, `mode()` (PUSH, PULL or BOTH), `verify(request)` (for a push: prove the delivery is the provider's, by the scheme the provider uses, an HMAC signature over the body or a client-certificate fingerprint, against the connection's secret), `fetch(window)` (for a pull), `readiness()` (asks the provider with the credentials the business holds, keeping READY, UNREACHABLE and REFUSED apart, as the sending side does), `respond(reference, status)` (slice 3). Drivers: **SIMULATED** (the platform's own network; what order-svc's `SimulatedTransport` delivers today), **KSEF** (the existing pull, moved behind the seam with no change of behaviour), and **ACCESS_POINT** (a Peppol access point behind the same bearer-facade shape the sending side uses: `GET /inbox?since=`, `GET /inbox/{ref}/document`, `POST /inbox/{ref}/response`, and a signed push), each **stub-tested for its exact request shape** with the driver kit's recording stub. France's platform is a fourth driver of the same shape, added the same way when its facade contract is fixed by the chosen provider. `einvoice_connections` (tenant, network, provider, provider account, the sealed credential, status, last delivery and last fetch). **The route becomes per connection:** `POST /e-invoices/inbound/{network}/{connectionId}`, where the connection names the business, so the receiver is no longer read out of the document and a document naming another buyer is `MISDIRECTED` as today. **The deployment-wide key route stays for existing simulated deliveries** and is marked deprecated with a dated `Sunset` header, as unversioned API aliases are; a business with a connection uses its own. The existing e-invoice intake is unchanged behind it.
  3. **Answers back to the sender (purchase-svc).** Append-only `supplier_einvoice_responses`: the document, the status kind (`RECEIVED`, `ACCEPTED`, `UNDER_QUERY`, `REFUSED`, `PAID`), a reason, who caused it (a person, or the system for a payment), and its own delivery state (QUEUED, SENT, ACKNOWLEDGED, FAILED) with attempts and the network's reference; unreachable networks are retried with back-off and never a refusal, a refusal by the network is FAILED and shown. **The driver maps a kind to the network's own vocabulary** (a Peppol invoice response carries a status code from its published list, France's platform a lifecycle code such as *refused*, *approved*, *disputed*, *payment transmitted*), and a network that has no such message answers `NOT_SUPPORTED`, which is recorded and is not a failure. **What triggers it:** refusing a document (the existing `POST …/{id}/refuse`) always sends `REFUSED` with the reason where the document came over a network that carries it, and where `E_INVOICE_REFUSAL_STATUS` binds it is **not switchable off**; approving a captured invoice and paying it send `ACCEPTED` and `PAID` only when the business switches on `einvoice_inbox_settings.respond_on_approval` / `respond_on_payment` (both off until set); a document from an upload has no network to answer and records nothing. `UNDER_QUERY` is sent by a person from the document's screen. `POST /e-invoices/{id}/responses` (one per kind and document unless it failed), `GET …/responses`.
  4. **Never turn a delivery away (purchase-svc).** A document **delivered by a network** is accepted whatever the pool holds: it is kept, its row carries `allowance_exceeded`, and the owner is told once (notification-svc `EINVOICE_ALLOWANCE_FULL`, in-app and email) so the plan can be raised or documents exported. An **upload** is still refused by `Entitlements.requireBytesWithin` as before. This **replaces**, for network deliveries only, the earlier rule that any document over the allowance is refused ([goods-receipt-detail](goods-receipt-detail.md), `documents.mb.max` pool; its text stands for uploads and receipt documents). Why: a mandated invoice that a supplier's provider was told to deliver, and the business's own plan refused, is a legal document lost, and a network's retries do not make a refused one arrive. Fails open when the allowance cannot be read, as limits do.
  5. **The archive (purchase-svc).** (a) A **guard**: a database trigger forbids updating a stored document's bytes, its sha256 or its received instant, and forbids deleting a row, so the original stays as it arrived (no function is used in a CHECK, so `scripts/backup-drill.sh` is unaffected; the drill's restore reads the table). (b) **The floor:** the retention period comes from tenant-svc's `retention_floors` for the business's country (class `TRANSACTIONS`, or an `E_INVOICE_ARCHIVE` figure where the law sets a longer one), read through the existing retention reader; purchase-svc keeps a document at least that long counted from its issue date and the business's own schedule may lengthen it, never shorten it. Nothing in purchase-svc deletes an e-invoice today; this makes that a tested rule, and declares the table `RETAIN` with the reason "legal retention" in `SubjectDataSpec` so a tenant erasure does not remove what a floor still holds. (c) **The export:** `GET /admin/e-invoices/archive?from&to` streams a package (each original document, its check report, its responses, its delivery and decision records, and a manifest of sha256 per file, built and verified by common-service's shared pure `ArchiveManifest`, which the sending side's archive uses too, see [e-invoices-and-e-reporting-for-sales](e-invoices-and-e-reporting-for-sales.md)); `POST /admin/e-invoices/archive/verify` recomputes every stored hash and reports any that differ (there should be none; a mismatch is `EINVOICE_ARCHIVE_TAMPERED`, raised as an alert). The export is `@SensitiveRead` and needs the permission above.
  6. **The queue and the alerts (purchase-svc).** The inbox list shows the age of the oldest document waiting for a person, and the metric `einvoices.inbox_waiting_age` (hours, sweep-only) lets the business draw a line; a connection with no delivery or fetch for the business's own chosen number of days is flagged on the duty checklist (`silent_days`, unset = never), the case *an inbox that will stay empty says so before a month of silence*.
  7. **The screens** (below).
- **Out, on purpose:**
  - **Finishing the last mile to a certified provider.** See the go-live list below: a contract, credentials, the provider's own conformance test and the mapping of the facade to the provider's real API are not code.
  - **Peppol network-registration lookups and sanctions screening.** Kept out by [supplier-assurance](supplier-assurance.md); the document's claims are compared with what the business holds.
  - **Refusing an unstructured invoice.** A warning only; goods and a liability are real.
  - **Issuing invoices and e-reporting.** The sending side, [e-invoices-and-e-reporting-for-sales](e-invoices-and-e-reporting-for-sales.md).
  - **A supplier portal or a self-billing flow.** Suppliers write nothing.
  - **Approval and payment terms negotiated over the network** (dispute workflows beyond `UNDER_QUERY`). The status is sent; the conversation is the supplier's and buyer's.
  - **Replacing the check rules.** `shared/einvoice`'s EN 16931, Peppol and FA(3) rules stand.

## Data and flow

- **Owned by** purchase-svc: `einvoice_connections` (tenant_id, network, provider, provider_account, sealed credential, status, timestamps; one live connection per network), `supplier_einvoice_responses` (append-only), columns on `supplier_einvoices`: `connection_id`, `warnings` (text list), `allowance_exceeded` (boolean); `einvoice_inbox_settings` gains `size_band`, `respond_on_approval`, `respond_on_payment`, `silent_days`; an immutability trigger on `supplier_einvoices`.
- **Owned by** tenant-svc: new rows in `legal_obligations` and `retention_floors` (data, with citations); nothing else.
- **Needs from other services:** obligations and floors through `Jurisdictions` and the retention reader (cached, **fail closed for the duty statement** so a business is never told "not required" because tenant-svc was down; **fail open for the warning**, never a refusal); the business's country, identity and electronic address through `TenantProfiles`; a provider's facade over HTTP through the driver with sealed credentials.
- **Events published:** `SupplierEInvoiceReceived` (`storeql.purchase.supplier-einvoice-received`: `eventId`, `tenantId`, `documentId`, `channel`, `supplierId?`, `status`, `occurredAt`) for notification-svc (the waiting-age alert and the allowance notice) and reporting-svc's counts; `SupplierEInvoiceResponded` (`eventId`, `tenantId`, `documentId`, `kind`, `deliveryState`, `occurredAt`) for the record. Consumers dedupe on `eventId`. A document received twice (same bytes) is the same document, as now.
- **Retryable writes** (Idempotency-Key): connection create and rotate, a response, an archive verify. A network delivery is idempotent by the document's bytes and the network's own reference.
- **New error codes:** `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_CONNECTION_UNKNOWN` 404; `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_CONNECTION_DUPLICATE` 409; `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_PROVIDER_UNKNOWN` 400; `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_PROVIDER_NOT_DEPLOYED` 409; `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_SIGNATURE_REFUSED` 401 (a push whose proof fails); `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_RESPONSE_KIND_INVALID` 400; `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_RESPONSE_NO_NETWORK` 409 (answering an upload); `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_RESPONSE_DUPLICATE` 409; `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_RESPONSE_REFUSAL_REQUIRED` 409 (switching off a refusal status where the law requires it); `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_ARCHIVE_PERIOD_INVALID` 400; `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_ARCHIVE_TAMPERED` 409 (verify's finding); `PURCHASE_EINVOICE_ARCHIVE_IMMUTABLE` 409 (an attempt to edit or delete a kept document); `BUSINESS_WIDE_ONLY` 403; the existing refusals of the intake (`400`, `413`, `415`, `422`, `409`) are unchanged.

## Money, time and limits

- **Currency:** none new; an invoice keeps its own, and the match is as today.
- **Ledger postings:** none new; capture posts as any invoice does.
- **Dates:** every record carries its UTC instant; the duty's dates and the retention floor are dates in the business's country; the archive's period is counted from the document's issue date; **no country, size band, format or zone is assumed** (an unset band reads as the earliest).
- **Plan limits:** the existing `documents.mb.max` pool ("Purchasing documents (MB)"); network deliveries are exempt from refusal as slice 4 says, uploads are not. No new key.

## Constraints

- **Existing tenants:** a business with no connection receives exactly as today (the deployment-key route and uploads); no warning appears in a country with no rule; the trigger allows every operation the code performs now (status changes) and forbids only edits to the stored original (`EInvoiceReceivedCompatibilityIT`, the migration test).
- **Golden rules 1 and 5:** provider credentials are sealed and never returned or logged; the provider's URL is deployment configuration, never in code.
- **The document is evidence:** what arrived is kept as it arrived; nothing rewrites it.
- **Consumers and retries:** delivery, fetch and response are idempotent; an unreachable network retries and a refused answer is recorded, neither loses a document.
- **Golden rule 15:** a push is verified before a byte of the body is read into the intake, as the deployment key is now.

## What only a real provider contract can finish (go-live items)

The user (or the deployment owner) must supply these; they are not code, and the platform will say so on the duty checklist until they exist:

1. A **contract with a certified provider** for each network the business uses: a Peppol access point (the business's receiving address registered in the network by that provider), an approved platform in France, and for Poland a KSeF token the business itself issues.
2. The provider's **credentials** and **push-signing secret or certificate**, sealed under the deployment key and entered by the owner; the deployment's `storeql.einvoice.*` base URLs and keys in `.env`.
3. The **mapping of the platform's facade** (the `GET /inbox`, `POST /inbox/{ref}/response` shapes the ACCESS_POINT driver is stub-tested against) to the provider's actual API, and the provider's own **conformance or certification test** passed with a real document.
4. Each country's **citations verified** by the platform's compliance owner before its data row goes live, and the business's **size band** where dates are staged.

  - *Edge:* an allowance full at a delivery, a mandate date exactly today, an unset size band, a network outage mid-response, a redelivered document, tenant-svc down — `EInvoiceAllowanceIT`, `EInvoiceDutyIT`, `EInvoiceResponseIT`.
  - *Audit:* the append-only response record and the manifest — `EInvoiceResponseIT.theRecordIsAppendOnly`, `EInvoiceArchiveIT.anExportIsProvable`.
