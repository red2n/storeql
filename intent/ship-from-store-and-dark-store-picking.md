## Problem

A shop that fulfils online orders has nowhere to see them as work. Picking is in one place (the Inventory tab's waves), "Mark fulfilled" is in another (the Orders list), and once an order is fulfilled it vanishes into history — though the parcel is still on the back-room shelf waiting for the courier, or the bag is behind the counter waiting for a shopper who has not been told it is ready. Nothing records that the courier took it or who collected it, and the shopper hears nothing after "your order is confirmed". A business that wants a site that only serves online orders — a dark store: stock, aisles, pickers, no shop floor — cannot say so: every store is a shop, so the storefront offers collection there, a till can be opened there, and staff have no way to run it as the fulfilment node it is.

## Outcome

Each store — shop or dark store — has a fulfilment queue: what waits to be picked, what is packed and waiting for the courier, what is ready and waiting to be collected, and what was handed over today. Picking leaves an order **fulfilled** exactly as it does now — the goods off the shelf, packed for the order — and the store then **hands it over** as a second, recorded step: *dispatched* to a named carrier with a reference, or *collected* by the shopper. The shopper is told when a collection is ready and when a delivery is on its way, and their order history says so. A business can declare a store a **dark store**: it holds stock, is replenished, delivers and is routed to like any shop; the storefront sells from it delivery-only, never offers collection there, and no till opens at it.

## Who and where

- **Personas** ([PRD §2](../PRD.md)): the storekeeper and cashier at a shop or a dark store, who pick, pack and hand over; the store manager, who sees the queue; the online shopper, who is told when to come and when to expect the courier; the owner, who declares a store dark.
- **Channels:** ONLINE orders for PICKUP or DELIVERY, at the back office (admin Fulfilment screen) and the storefront (what the shopper is told and sees). A till sale (INSTORE) is handed over when it is paid and takes no part.
- **Scope:** per store. The queue is one store's; a handover is recorded at the order's store by staff assigned there.
- **Roles that can write:** handover — any member of staff assigned to the order's store (CASHIER, STOREKEEPER, MANAGER, OWNER; a customer never); the store type — OWNER, MANAGER (as store fields are today).
- **Sandbox tenant:** behaves the same (notification-svc suppresses what leaves, as it does for every message there).

## Scope

- **In:**
  - **The handover, a recorded step after fulfilment.** `POST /orders/{id}/dispatch {carrier, reference?, parcels?}` for a DELIVERY order and `POST /orders/{id}/collect {collectedBy?}` for a PICKUP order, each once, only on a FULFILLED order, by staff at the order's store; appended to `order_handovers` and to the order's status history; announced as `OrderDispatched` / `OrderCollected`. The order's answer carries `handover {kind, carrier, reference, parcels, collectedBy, at, by}`; the lists take `handover=PENDING|DONE` and `fulfilmentType=` so a queue can be read.
  - **The shopper told.** `ORDER_READY_FOR_COLLECTION` when a pickup order becomes FULFILLED (from `OrderFulfilled`, which gains the buyer's ids), `ORDER_DISPATCHED` with the carrier and reference at dispatch — both catalogued message types with templates a business may reword, email and push as `ORDER_CONFIRMED` is. Both events join the webhook catalogue.
  - **The Fulfilment screen** (admin, per store): *To pick* (the confirmed orders waiting, from inventory-svc's list, with a way to the Picking tab), *Packed — awaiting courier* with **Dispatch** (carrier, reference, parcels), *Ready for collection* with **Collected** (who, optional), *Handed over today*. The Orders list's "Hand over" dialog reads *Picked & packed* for an online order, since the handover is now its own step. The storefront's order history reads *Ready for collection*, *Packed*, *On its way · DPD 1Z…*, *Collected*.
  - **The dark store.** `DARK_STORE` as a third store type in tenant-svc (the Stores screen offers it); `TenantProfiles.Stores` knows which stores are dark; the storefront lists a dark store delivery-only (`/storefront/stores` and `/storefront/config` say `type` and `pickupOffered: false`; the cart offers no collection there); order-svc refuses a PICKUP order and a POS order at a dark store; iam-svc refuses a till session at one (it learns the type from `StoreStatusChanged`, which gains `type`). Routing, waves, depot replenishment, cross-docking and transfers treat it as the shop it is.
- **Out, on purpose:**
  - **Carrier integration, labels, tracking and delivery confirmation** — the carrier is a name and the reference is typed; the parcel's journey after the door is *Transport and route planning*, its own row.
  - **Dispatching a part-picked order** — a delivery is dispatched whole, once FULFILLED (see open questions); closing a short line is *Substitutions for out-of-stock online lines*, the next row.
  - **A collection code or identity check at the counter** — who collected is a name typed by staff; a code in the ready message is a small follow-up if a business asks.
  - **Uncollected orders lapsing** (a pickup nobody collects in N days, cancelled and restocked) — its own sweeper, later.
  - **Store fulfilment capacity** (how many online orders a shop takes a day, and routing around it) — a later refinement of orchestration.
  - **Picking itself** — waves already build, direct and complete a pick at any store; a dark store is picked by the same waves. Handheld scanning and pick-to-tote are device rows.
  - **Hiding warehouses from the storefront** — today's storefront lists every active store; a warehouse listed there is an existing oddity, noted, not this row's.

## Data and flow

- **Owned by** order-svc: `order_handovers` (append-only; one per order — `UNIQUE (tenant_id, order_id)`; `kind` DISPATCHED | COLLECTED, `carrier`, `reference`, `parcels`, `collected_by`, `handed_by`, `handed_at`); a status-history row per handover ("dispatched: DPD 1Z…", "collected by …"). `orders.status` is not changed by a handover: FULFILLED stays the moment the goods leave the shelf and the sale is recognised, and every consumer that keys on it keeps working. tenant-svc: `stores.type` admits `DARK_STORE`.
- **Needs from other services:** order-svc reads the store's type through `TenantProfiles.Stores` (REST, cached, never joined) to refuse a pickup or a till sale at a dark store; iam-svc learns the type from tenant-svc's `StoreStatusChanged` (gaining `type`), as it learns the status; notification-svc resolves the shopper's address and language through customer-svc as it does for a confirmation; the Fulfilment screen's *To pick* list is inventory-svc's `GET /admin/inventory/waves/awaiting`.
- **Events published:** `OrderDispatched {eventId, tenantId, orderId, storeId, customerId, loginId, carrier, reference, parcels}` and `OrderCollected {eventId, tenantId, orderId, storeId, customerId, loginId, collectedBy}` (topics `storeql.order.order-dispatched`, `storeql.order.order-collected`) on the handover's transaction; `OrderFulfilled` gains `customerId` and `loginId`; `StoreStatusChanged` gains `type`. Consumers: notification-svc (`OrderFulfilled` → ready for collection, once, only when `status` is FULFILLED and `fulfilmentType` PICKUP; `OrderDispatched` → on its way), iam-svc (`StoreStatusChanged.type`), webhooks.
- **Retryable writes** (Idempotency-Key): none new — a handover is idempotent by state: a second dispatch or collection of the same order is `409 ORDER_ALREADY_HANDED_OVER` rather than a second record.
- **New error codes:** `ORDER_NOT_PICKED` 409 (dispatch or collect on an order that is not FULFILLED — still being picked, cancelled, or a part-picked order); `ORDER_HANDOVER_KIND_MISMATCH` 409 (dispatch on a pickup, collect on a delivery, either on a till sale); `ORDER_ALREADY_HANDED_OVER` 409; `ORDER_PICKUP_NOT_OFFERED` 409 (a PICKUP order at a dark store); `ORDER_NO_TILL_AT_DARK_STORE` 409 (a POS order at a dark store); iam-svc `POS_STORE_HAS_NO_TILL` 409; `VALIDATION_FAILED` 400 (a dispatch with no carrier, a carrier or reference over 80 characters, parcels below 1); `403 STORE_ACCESS_DENIED` for staff not assigned to the order's store; `404 ORDER_NOT_FOUND` for another tenant's order.

## Money, time and limits

- **Currency:** none; nothing here is priced. Revenue is recognised at FULFILLED as today; a handover moves no money.
- **Ledger postings:** none new.
- **Dates:** `handed_at` UTC, shown in the store's time on the screen.
- **Plan limits:** none.

## Constraints

Database-per-service: order-svc asks tenant-svc's profile for the store type and never its table; iam-svc projects the type from an event; notification-svc reads the buyer from the event and their address from customer-svc. Event consumers idempotent: a redelivered `OrderFulfilled` or `OrderDispatched` sends one message (notification-svc's `notifyOnce` on the event id); `OrderFulfilled` is emitted once per handover and the ready message fires only on the one that makes the order FULFILLED. Append-only stays append-only: `order_handovers` and `order_status_history`. Every existing consumer of order events keeps its one meaning of FULFILLED. The handover is read by store scope like the order's other writes (`requireStoreAccess`); a shopper reads their own handover on their own order and nothing of anyone else's. The store type is refused, not defaulted, when unknown (`TENANT_STORE_TYPE_INVALID` stays); a dark store is a shop to routing (`Routing` candidates exclude warehouses only), to depot replenishment (`isWarehouse` false → served) and to cross-docking.

## Open questions

- [x] **What is the handover?** Recommended: keep FULFILLED as *picked and packed* — exactly what a completed wave leaves behind, the goods off the shelf and the sale recognised — and add the handover as a second, explicit, recorded step: *Dispatched* (carrier and reference typed) for a delivery, *Collected* for a pickup; the shopper is told *ready for collection* when a pickup order is fully picked and *on its way* with the carrier and reference at dispatch, and nothing at collection (they were there). The alternative is to treat today's FULFILLED as the handover and record nothing more, which leaves the courier's collection and the shopper's collection unrecorded and the shopper untold. → **a recorded second step: FULFILLED is picked and packed; Dispatched / Collected after it; the shopper told ready and on its way** (the user, 2026-09-25)
- [x] **Is a part-picked delivery dispatched in part?** Recommended: no — an order is dispatched whole, once every line is picked (FULFILLED); a short line waits for the next pick or is closed by the substitutions row, and the shopper gets one parcel and one message. The alternative dispatches what is packed and leaves the rest as a second shipment, which is multi-parcel shipping — Transport's row. → **no: dispatched whole, once every line is picked** (the user, 2026-09-25)
- [x] **Does the storefront sell from a dark store?** Recommended: yes, delivery-only — it is listed with the shops (its prices and stock are what serve the shopper), collection is never offered there and refused if asked, and no till opens at it; a business with only dark stores is a working online shop. The alternative hides a dark store from the storefront altogether, which leaves that business with nothing to browse. → **yes, delivery-only; collection refused there; no till** (the user, 2026-09-25)
- [x] **Who hands an order over?** Recommended: any member of staff assigned to the order's store — a cashier hands a bag across the counter and a storekeeper hands parcels to the courier; no management role and no `stock.transfer` needed. The alternative confines it to storekeepers and managers, as building a wave is. → **any member of staff assigned to the order's store** (the user, 2026-09-25)

## Decisions

- **FULFILLED is picked and packed; the handover is a second step, and `orders.status` never moves for it.** A completed wave already fulfils an order the shopper has not yet received, so FULFILLED already meant "off the shelf" for waved orders; the handover is recorded beside the order (`order_handovers`, one per order, append-only, plus a status-history line) rather than as new statuses, so every consumer that keys on FULFILLED — refunds, returns, tombstones, reporting — keeps working unchanged. The Orders list's dialog was renamed *Picked & packed* to match.
- **One handover per order, decided by the unique row.** `UNIQUE (tenant_id, order_id)` is the guard against two members of staff at once; the service's read-before-write only makes the message better (`ORDER_ALREADY_HANDED_OVER`).
- **The kind follows the order, never the caller.** A dispatch is only for an ONLINE DELIVERY and a collection only for an ONLINE PICKUP (`ORDER_HANDOVER_KIND_MISMATCH`); a till sale has neither — it is handed over when it is paid.
- **Whole, or not yet.** A part-picked (PARTIALLY_FULFILLED) delivery is `ORDER_NOT_PICKED`: one parcel, one message; a short line waits for the next pick or the substitutions row.
- **Any member of staff assigned to the order's store hands over**, cashiers included, through `requireStoreAccess`; the shared filter already keeps a shopper out of the mutation.
- **The shopper is told twice, and only when it means something.** `OrderFulfilled` gained the buyer's ids (`customerId`, `loginId`, JSON null when there is none) so notification-svc needs no call back to order-svc; *ready for collection* fires only on the event whose `status` is FULFILLED with `fulfilmentType` PICKUP and `channel` ONLINE — a part handover, a delivery, a till sale, a guest and an event from before those fields existed send nothing; *on its way* fires on `OrderDispatched`, naming the carrier and, in a template section, the reference only when there is one. The three order messages share one path (`OrderMessages.tell`): email in the shopper's language, a push to a registered phone, each once per event.
- **Only the two new events name the handover.** `OrderConfirmed`, `OrderFulfilled` and the rest are unchanged in meaning; `OrderDispatched` and `OrderCollected` join the webhook catalogue, and `OrderFulfilled`'s catalogue line now says *picked and packed*.
- **The queue is the orders list with two filters**, not a new resource: `fulfilmentType=` and `handover=PENDING|DONE` (an `EXISTS` on the handover row, tenant-scoped in the subquery), and every order and summary carries `handover`. What waits to be picked stays inventory-svc's list, which the Fulfilment screen only counts.
- **A dark store is tenant-svc's third type, and every reader treats it as a shop unless told otherwise.** `TenantProfiles.Stores` gained `dark` (`isDark`); routing, depot replenishment and cross-docking keep asking `isWarehouse`, so a dark store is served, routed to and picked like any shop. Its refusals live where the thing refused is owned: order-svc refuses the pickup and the till sale (`ORDER_PICKUP_NOT_OFFERED`, `ORDER_NO_TILL_AT_DARK_STORE`), reading the type through the cached profile and **taking an unreadable answer for a shop** (routing's rule: an outage never refuses a sale); iam-svc refuses the till session (`POS_STORE_HAS_NO_TILL`) from its own `store_types` projection, fed by `StoreStatusChanged`, which now carries `type` — an event-carried type rather than a call to tenant-svc from the identity service, and an iam-svc-only table rather than a column on the shared `store_status` projection three services each create.
- **The storefront lists a dark store delivery-only** (`type`, `pickupOffered` on `/storefront/stores` and `/storefront/config`): the cart hides collection and reads *Delivery only from this shop*, the switcher names it *· delivery only*, and the till's store picker drops it — the server refuses anyway. A warehouse listed on the storefront is an oddity noted, not fixed here.
- **Tenant isolation is tested the hard way** (the user asked for it on this row): another business's owner, manager, cashier and storekeeper — including one whose store scope names our store's id — read nothing (404, never 403), list nothing, dispatch and collect nothing, and the database shows nothing moved and nothing written under them; the iam-svc test feeds the status projection too, so the other tenant's cashier is refused by the real check, not by a fail-open default.
- **Migration numbers are read with `sort -V`.** iam-svc had V11–V14 (an `ls | tail` sorted V10 before V7 and hid them), so the store-types migration is V15.
