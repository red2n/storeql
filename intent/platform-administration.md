## Problem

One role, `PLATFORM_ADMIN`, holds every cross-business power and no two people ever have to agree: it can suspend a business, change its plan, rotate the token signing key, republish every business's currency and sweep every till session with one call each. Nothing separates a support person who only needs to look from the person who can act. Suspending a business does not stop its staff for up to fifteen seconds and only if the gateway can reach tenant-svc (it fails open); a login disabled or a session ended stays usable for its access token's minutes. A platform administrator cannot set a business up for a prospect, cannot erase a business on a legal request outside the leaving-notice path, and there is no single place that says who did what across the platform: three trails, one of them (identity) unread until wave 1, another (till and staff actions) order-svc's alone. On the money side a business that downgrades gets nothing back for the unused part, dunning speaks only by email, and an owner is never told they are at 80 % of a limit unless they open the usage screen.

## Outcome

- **Platform staff have tiers.** A support person reads everything and changes nothing; an operator runs day-to-day (suspend, reactivate, plan changes, onboarding, unlock, second-factor reset); an administrator does all that plus the irreversible, platform-wide and access-granting actions, and those need a second administrator to agree.
- **A suspended business stops at the gateway within a second or two** of the decision, not after a cache; a login that is disabled or signed out is refused at the gateway at once too.
- **A platform operator sets a business up for someone** and the future owner receives a link to set their password.
- **An administrator erases a business, or one person, on a legal request**, only when a second administrator agrees, with the same evidence the leaving path already produces.
- **A downgrade may take effect now and credit the unused time**; dunning reaches the owner in the app and, where set, by text; an owner is told when they reach 80 % and 100 % of an allowance.
- **One audit view** answers "who did what in this business, in date order" across identity, set-up, till and the other trails.

## Who and where

- **Personas** ([PRD §2](../PRD.md)): the platform administrator, the platform operator and platform support (new tiers, below), the owner (receives notices, sees the audit view of their own business), the prospect being onboarded.
- **Channels:** platform console (all the platform screens) · back-office (the owner's audit view and notices) · the gateway.
- **Scope:** platform-wide for the tiers; the audit view is per business for owners and managers (business-wide only, `BusinessWide.require`, with a store-held manager seeing only their stores' entries and the business-wide ones, as tenant-svc's audit does today).
- **Roles that can write:** by tier (below). OWNER and MANAGER keep what they have; nothing on this page lets a business act on another.
- **Sandbox tenant:** on-behalf onboarding can make a sandbox pair; erasure and suspension treat a sandbox like any business (a sandbox's erasure needs no second administrator: it holds no real data, `TenantProfiles.Profile.sandbox()`).

## Scope

- **In:** slices in build order:
  1. **Suspension and denial at once (gateway, iam-svc, tenant-svc).** The gateway subscribes to `storeql.tenant.tenant-status-changed` and to a new `storeql.iam.login-access-denied`, each replica with a group of its own (so every replica sees every event, unlike a work queue), starting from the latest offset. On an event it sets its tenant cache entry straight to the new status (no waiting for a TTL) and adds a denied user to a small in-memory-plus-Redis deny list for the remaining life of an access token (15 minutes, the token lifetime, held in one constant with the token issuer). A request from a suspended business is refused `403 TENANT_INACTIVE` (the code sign-in already uses); a denied user `401 SESSION_REVOKED`. The 15-second polling stays as the **backstop** for a missed event and is what covers a gateway that has just started; the gateway's fail-open on an unreadable status stays (a platform outage must not take every business's tills down). The gateway's readiness does **not** wait on Kafka (the feed is best-effort; it reports its lag as a metric `gateway.status_feed.lag_seconds` and the TTL backstop bounds the damage), an exception to "ready checks DB + Kafka + config" that is stated here because the gateway is a platform service that must keep passing traffic.
  2. **Tiers and the platform's own action trail (iam-svc, common-web, tenant-svc, app).** Three tiers: `SUPPORT` (reads), `OPERATOR` (SUPPORT plus suspend / reactivate, plan changes, on-behalf onboarding, unlock, second-factor reset for staff, and the sweeps that fix stuck things), `ADMINISTRATOR` (all, plus the actions of slices 3 and 5, granting tiers, signing-key rotation, currency republishing). **Existing code keeps working:** the role in the token stays `PLATFORM_ADMIN` for every platform login (the 49 `requireAnyRole("PLATFORM_ADMIN")` sites and the many `OWNER/MANAGER/PLATFORM_ADMIN` lists need not change), and a new token claim `ptier` says which tier. The shared authorization filter in common-web (which already gates every service) refuses any write (anything but GET, HEAD, OPTIONS) from a `SUPPORT` token in every service, and a new `TenantContext.requirePlatformTier(Tier.OPERATOR)` (and `ADMINISTRATOR`) guards the finer actions. The tier capability table is one pure class (`PlatformTiers`) with a test that lists every `/platform/**` route and says which tier it needs, so a new route without a decision fails the build. A token with no `ptier` is treated as `SUPPORT` (fail to the least). Platform staff sign in at `/auth/platform-login`, which today accepts only the administrator; it now accepts every platform login, and **every platform tier must have a second factor** (`mfaEnrolmentRequired` on first sign-in, as the administrator has). The administrator makes and changes platform staff (`POST/PATCH/DELETE /platform/staff`, in iam-svc, `ADMINISTRATOR` and, for granting the ADMINISTRATOR tier, a second administrator: slice 3). **The platform's own trail** is a new append-only `platform_audit` in tenant-svc: every write by a platform login, in any service, is recorded **by the executing service in the same transaction as the action** through `PlatformAudit.record(tx, …)` (common-service), which puts a `PlatformActionRecorded` event (actor, tier, route class, tenant named, reason when the endpoint takes one, the approval id if any) in that service's own outbox; tenant-svc appends it; the console reads it. So "who suspended that business" is answered without reading three trails. **Not a generic filter:** a filter runs after the commit, so a crash between the two would lose exactly the entry an audit exists to keep (this page first proposed the filter announcing it; corrected 2026-09-30). The shared filter enforces only the tier; a test, like `PlatformTiersTest`, lists every platform write route and fails when one has no `PlatformAudit.record` call.
  3. **A second administrator for what cannot be undone ([approvals](approvals.md)).** The approvals page designs the mechanism once; this page names what it guards, each a **platform-scope** approval action (an approval with no business: [approvals](approvals.md) slice 10 adds the `scope` to the same block; decided by a different `ADMINISTRATOR` than the requester, never the requester): `platform.administrator-grant` (making another ADMINISTRATOR), `platform.tenant-erase` (slice 5), `platform.shopper-erase` (slice 5), `platform.tenant-mass-suspend` (suspending more than one business in one action), `platform.signing-key-rotate`, `platform.currency-republish`. `POST /platform/tenants/republish-currency`, the signing-key rotation and mass suspension become **requests that wait for an approval**; the ordinary single suspend / reactivate stays with one operator (it is reversible and urgent). **The platform-wide POS sweep** (`POST /auth/pos/sessions/sweep`) is idempotent and non-destructive (it expires sessions already idle), so it takes no approval, only the OPERATOR tier, a written reason and a fresh second factor, and is rate-limited ([approvals](approvals.md) lists it as not an approval). While the platform has only one active administrator, the **irreversible** actions (`platform.administrator-grant`, `platform.tenant-erase`, `platform.shopper-erase`) are refused `409 PLATFORM_SECOND_ADMINISTRATOR_REQUIRED` (with the break-glass in [sign-in-protection](sign-in-protection.md) as the only unilateral path, which is audited and announced); the wide but recoverable ones (`platform.signing-key-rotate`, `platform.currency-republish`, `platform.tenant-mass-suspend`) proceed on a written reason and a fresh second factor, because refusing key rotation on a one-administrator platform would stop the remedy for the very incident that caused it ([approvals](approvals.md), "Platform-scope approvals", where this is settled). The console warns when there is one. **Dependency:** [approvals](approvals.md) slice 10 (an approval without a business) is written and designed there; this slice waits for it and the slice-2 tiers still ship.
  4. **On-behalf onboarding (tenant-svc, iam-svc, notification-svc).** `POST /platform/tenants` (OPERATOR; `Idempotency-Key`) takes what the business's own wizard takes (name, country, home currency, time zone, first store, plan or trial) plus the future owner's email and name, and does what self-signup does through the same service code (`OnboardingResource` and `AuthService.registerBusiness` internals): an owner login with **no password**, the tenant, its first store and default zone, the owner's assignment, the plan. The owner is sent a set-your-password link through the password-reset machinery (`PasswordResetRequested`, 30 minutes, once, hash only), worded "Your business was set up by <platform>", and the first sign-in makes them a second factor if their business's policy asks. Nothing in the body is defaulted from a country: the operator must give the currency, time zone and countries (`400 ONBOARD_PROFILE_REQUIRED` naming what is missing). The record says `onboardedBy` (the platform login) and `onboardedAt`, and the trail has `TENANT_ONBOARDED_BY_PLATFORM`. A retry with the same `Idempotency-Key` answers with the first tenant and mints nothing (catalogue ONB-112 is the self-service twin of this).
  5. **Erasure on demand (tenant-svc, every service, iam-svc, customer-svc).** See "Erasure" below.
  6. **Billing follow-through (tenant-svc, notification-svc).** *Downgrade credit:* `POST` the plan change with `when: NOW` on a **lower** plan is now allowed: it is billed as the upgrade is, through `Proration` with the credit of what was billed for the unused days and the debit of the new plan for them, and the net, when negative, is a **credit note that stays as account credit** applied to the next invoice(s); it is refunded in cash only by a person with the platform's payment-reversal action, never automatically (`when: PERIOD_END` stays the default and stays as it is, for the reason its code comment gives: it "gives back something already paid for"). A business in `PAST_DUE` gets no credit (the upgrade's rule 3 applies unchanged); annual and monthly plans differ only in the days in the period; rounding to the currency's minor units, tax reversed pro rata with the credit note (the platform's own VAT rules, `BillingTax`). A downgrade now is refused `409 PLAN_DOWNGRADE_OVER_LIMIT` naming what exceeds the new plan's allowance (stores, staff, images), so the business is never below its limits on the day. *Dunning channels:* each dunning stage gains a channel list (email always; the owner's in-app notice; SMS where the stage says so and the owner has a phone read in the business's own countries and the deployment has an SMS driver), set by the administrator with the policy (`DUNNING_POLICY_INVALID` for an unknown channel). notification-svc sends each channel once per notice (`DunningNoticeIssued` already carries the notice id; the channel list rides on it). The sandbox gets in-app only. *Usage warning: owned by [exception-alerts](exception-alerts.md) (metric `usage.share`), not designed here.* When tenant-svc raises a `usage_alerts` row it raises one `ExceptionAlertRaised` in the same transaction; the owner reads it in the alert inbox and by email, once per threshold per period (the alert row is unique already, which is what makes it once), the sandbox in-app only. The 80 and 100 thresholds are the ones the platform already has. (This page first proposed a `UsageThresholdReached` event and its own consumer; withdrawn so a business has one inbox.)
  7. **One audit view (reporting-svc, every service that keeps a trail).** See "One audit view" below.
- **Out, on purpose:**
  - **Approval for a single suspension or reactivation.** They are reversible and often urgent (fraud, non-payment); a second person there would cost more than it protects. The reason is required and the trail is complete instead.
  - **Making the platform-wide POS sweep or a business's own actions wait for approval.** They are not irreversible.
  - **Time-boxed elevation (an operator asking to be an administrator for an hour).** A separate identity-governance feature; the tiers and the approval do the job here.
  - **Cash refunds of downgrade credit as a feature.** It is a credit note; a cash refund is a payment reversal, which already exists as a platform action.
  - **SMS through a specific carrier.** SMS is already a driver behind an interface (`SmsProvider`, `Providers.sms()` in notification-svc, with a simulated driver and stub-backed tests of each real driver's exact request shape, the accounting-connectors pattern), credentials sealed from `.env`; which carrier a deployment uses is its choice. This page adds no driver. A dunning text is the *platform's* own message to a business's owner: it uses that same provider and is **not** counted against the business's `sms.per-month` allowance ([shopper-notices](shopper-notices.md)), which meters the business's texts to its shoppers.
  - **An audit event stream that copies every trail into one table.** Considered and rejected (see the audit decision); each service keeps its own trail, and the merge happens at read.
  - **Cross-business admin of a business's own settings by support.** The support tier reads; it never impersonates a business's staff.

### Erasure

- **A business, on demand.** `POST /platform/tenants/{id}/erasure {reason, legalRef}` (ADMINISTRATOR, `Idempotency-Key`) files an approval `platform.tenant-erase`; once a **different** administrator approves it, tenant-svc starts the erasure exactly as the leaving path does: the same `TenantDataErasureDue` (marked `origin: ON_DEMAND`, with the reference), the same `TenantDataErasureHandler` in all twelve services, the same `TenantDataErased` evidence with counts. Nothing is re-implemented; only the notice period is skipped, and only these guards are added:
  - The business must already be **suspended** (`409 TENANT_ERASURE_NEEDS_SUSPENSION`): erasure is never the first thing that happens to a trading business.
  - A **retention hold** (`RetentionResource`, a legal hold the business or the platform placed) blocks it (`409 TENANT_ERASURE_HELD`) until released; the business's obligation to keep records for a statutory period is its own retention setting and the same guard the leaving path applies. Records the *platform* must itself keep as the seller of its own service (the invoices and payments it issued to the business, and its own action trail) are retained by tenant-svc with the business's name and address, and nothing else; the erasure certificate says so.
  - The owner is **offered the export first** (the existing `/admin/tenant-data` export) and told the date; an administrator may waive that only for a legal reason recorded in `legalRef` (a court order, an authority's demand), never for convenience.
  - When every service has answered `TenantDataErased`, tenant-svc produces the **certificate**: per service the tables and row counts erased, the instant, the two administrators, the reference. It is kept in `tenant_erasure_certificates` (append-only, no personal data) and readable in the console; the owner is sent it if their address is still known (it is the last message to it).
  - Erasing twice finds nothing the second time (the handler's own guarantee); a service that fails is retried by Kafka and the run stays open in the console as "waiting for <service>".
- **One person, on demand.** Two cases, because there are two controllers.
  - *A business's customer: owned by [privacy-requests](privacy-requests.md) slice 4,* which defines `SubjectDataSpec`, the per-service export sections, and the acknowledgement every service gives (`SubjectErasureCompleted`; this page first called it `SubjectErased` and named cart-svc, payment-svc and reporting-svc; they are all in that page's list now). Nothing is designed twice here.
  - *A shopper's platform login* (the login itself belongs to no business, so the platform is its controller). A shopper deletes their own (`POST /auth/delete-account`, exists). On a legal request an ADMINISTRATOR files `POST /platform/shoppers/{loginId}/erasure {reason, legalRef}` and, once a different administrator approves (`platform.shopper-erase`), iam-svc anonymises the login and its sessions and publishes `ShopperLoginErased`; cart-svc deletes the carts, notification-svc purges the message copies (`AccountDeleted` already reaches it), and each business's customer-svc **unlinks** the login from the customer record (login id nulled) but does **not** erase the business's own customer record, which is the business's data and its decision (the business is told through the console's notice; it erases through its own action). The evidence is the same `SubjectErasureCompleted` per service (`subjectKind` SHOPPER_LOGIN), declared in each service's `SubjectDataSpec`.
- **What the platform does not do:** it never reaches into a business's customer records on its own initiative. On a legal request to erase a person from a specific business, an ADMINISTRATOR may open and verify a request in customer-svc and honour it there ([privacy-requests](privacy-requests.md): no export or erasure without a verified request); the business is told, and it is in the platform trail.

### One audit view

- **The shape.** Each service that keeps a trail answers the same small read, defined once in common-web: `GET /admin/<service>/audit-feed?from&to&after&limit&store` returning `{items: [{id, source, type, actorId, subjectId, storeId, occurredAt, summary}], nextCursor}`, newest first, `summary` only from an allow-list of safe words (the security-events read's rule: never an address typed at a sign-in, a secret, a hash, a token or a link), management only, store-held managers seeing their stores and the business-wide entries. The trails that exist: iam-svc `audit_log` (already read by `/auth/admin/security-events`), tenant-svc `tenant_admin_audit`, order-svc's till and staff-action trail (`/admin/audit/events`), and any other service's append-only log added later; a service adds the feed when it adds a trail.
- **Who aggregates: reporting-svc.** It is already the platform's read-side service and holds no business data of its own to protect from a merge. It fans out over the feeds with the caller's business (never a body field), merges by `(occurredAt, id)`, and returns one timeline `GET /admin/reports/audit` with a composite cursor (one position per source, so paging loses and repeats nothing); a source that cannot be reached is named in `meta.unavailable` and the rest still answer (a partial audit that says so beats none). It stores nothing: the rejected alternative was a projection of every trail by events (a second copy of sensitive rows, an event to publish on every append, a backfill for each service), which the merge-at-read avoids; the cost is that a very old range costs one query per service, which the mandatory `from`/`to` window (no default wider than 31 days) bounds.
- **Platform view.** An administrator or support reads the same for one business (`?tenantId=`), plus the platform's own trail (`platform_audit`, slice 2) at `GET /platform/audit`.

## Data and flow

- **Owned by tenant-svc:**
  - `platform_audit` (append-only): id, actor login, tier, action key, tenant named (nullable), reason, approval id (nullable), the request's `requestId`, at. Written from `PlatformActionRecorded`.
  - `tenants` gains `onboarded_by` (nullable), `onboarded_at`; `tenant_erasures` (id, tenant, origin `LEAVING` or `ON_DEMAND`, reason, legal ref, requested by, approved by, started at, completed at; one per erasure) and `tenant_erasure_certificates` (append-only, per service counts).
  - Billing: `subscription_credits` (append-only credit-note ledger per business: amount NUMERIC(18,4), currency, source invoice, applied against which invoice, at), `dunning_stage_channels` on the dunning policy.
- **Owned by iam-svc:** platform staff logins with `ptier` (`platform_staff`: user id, tier, granted by, at; append-only history of grants), `login_access_denials` (user, until, reason, at; append-only, what feeds the deny event), `shopper_erasures` (evidence). Audit codes for tiers, denial and shopper erasure.
- **Owned by reporting-svc:** nothing stored for the audit view.
- **Needs from other services:** the gateway reads events only (no REST to tenant-svc for the fast path; the existing poll stays as the backstop). Every service answers the audit feed and, for erasure, the two existing / new event shapes. The console reads other services' answers through the gateway.
- **Events published (outbox, `eventId`, idempotent consumers):** `TenantStatusChanged` (exists; the gateway is a new consumer), `LoginAccessDenied` (`storeql.iam.login-access-denied`: user, tenant, until; gateway), `PlatformActionRecorded` (`storeql.platform.action-recorded`; tenant-svc), `TenantDataErasureDue` gains `origin` and `legalRef` (additive), `ShopperLoginErased` (`storeql.iam.shopper-login-erased`: cart-svc, customer-svc, notification-svc), `SubjectErasureCompleted` (each service; customer-svc and tenant-svc collect; defined in [privacy-requests](privacy-requests.md)), `DunningNoticeIssued` gains `channels` (additive; notification-svc).
- **Retryable writes (Idempotency-Key):** on-behalf onboarding, the erasure requests, the plan change (already), staff grants.
- **New error codes:** `403 PLATFORM_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (names the tier needed), `409 PLATFORM_SECOND_ADMINISTRATOR_REQUIRED`, (the action filed and waiting is the approvals page's `202` with the request; this page's `409 PLATFORM_APPROVAL_PENDING` is withdrawn), `403 APPROVAL_SELF_DECISION` (the approvals page's code; a person cannot approve their own request; this page's `PLATFORM_APPROVAL_SAME_ADMINISTRATOR` is withdrawn), `400 ONBOARD_PROFILE_REQUIRED`, `409 TENANT_ERASURE_NEEDS_SUSPENSION`, `409 TENANT_ERASURE_HELD`, `409 PLAN_DOWNGRADE_OVER_LIMIT`, `401 SESSION_REVOKED` (gateway).
- **Settings (owner, default):** all tiers, thresholds and windows are platform constants already or fixed by this page; no business policy number is introduced. The audit view's window cap (31 days) is a technical bound on the merge's cost (setting `storeql.reporting.audit.max-window-days`, owner reporting-svc). SMS driver and carrier credentials are deployment values in `.env`.

## Money, time and limits

- **Currency:** the credit note is in the currency the invoice was billed in (the business's plan currency); nothing is converted, no rate assumed. **Ledger postings:** the platform's own books (tenant-svc billing): a downgrade credit is a credit note reversing the tax pro rata, held as account credit (a liability to the business) and consumed by the next invoice; the platform's chart is its own and the postings are tested in `BillingIT`. None in a business's ledger.
- **Dates:** UTC throughout; proration counts days in the billing period's own terms as `Proration` already does; the erasure certificate and the trails are UTC; the access-token denial lasts the token lifetime constant.
- **Plan limits:** none new. The downgrade guard *reads* the new plan's limits from tenant-svc's own counts and the other owners' usage (`Entitlements`-style reads: products from product-svc, images and documents from theirs), refusing conservatively when a count cannot be read (`409 PLAN_DOWNGRADE_OVER_LIMIT` with `unverified: [...]`, never assumed under).

## Constraints

- **Golden rules:** 1 (erasure is each service erasing its own data on an event, the audit view is a merge of REST answers, never a join); 2 (the gateway stays the only door); 3 (a business's audit view is its own, the tenant from the token; platform reads name the tenant explicitly and are in the platform trail); 6 and 7 (every announcement through the outbox, consumers idempotent on `eventId`); 8 (every trail here is append-only; the erasure erases data, not the trails' record that it happened, which carries no personal data); 12 (see the gateway readiness exception); 14 (UTC).
- **Token conventions:** RS256, key by `kid`, no service handed a signing secret: `ptier` is a claim iam-svc signs; the gateway trusts nothing else. Rotating the signing key is an approval-guarded action that never exposes a private half.
- **Second factors (20.12):** every platform login has one; the second administrator's approval is itself a signed-in action after a second factor.
- **Release and supply chain:** the gateway's new Kafka client is a dependency raised once in the parent pom through Helidon's managed version; `scripts/vuln-scan.sh deps` must pass; the SMS driver adds no dependency (HTTP through the existing client).
- **Backups and restore:** `tenant_erasures` and the certificates must survive a restore and are in `scripts/backup-drill.sh`'s check; an erasure that ran after a backup is **re-applied** on restore (the drill's step: restore, then replay `TenantDataErasureDue` for every completed on-demand erasure newer than the backup) so a restore never brings back erased data. This is a real constraint on `docs/BACKUP-AND-RESTORE.md` and is added there when built.
- **Existing businesses and tokens:** a `PLATFORM_ADMIN` token without `ptier` (issued before this ships) is read as `SUPPORT` for 15 minutes until it is refreshed, then carries `ADMINISTRATOR` for the existing administrator through the migration that seeds `platform_staff` from the existing administrator; nobody loses their power.
- **Location-neutral:** no country is assumed by the on-behalf onboarding, the SMS (numbers read through `PhoneNumbers` in the business's countries), the erasure legal reference (a free reference, not a statute name) or the notices (language from the owner's own setting, English only as the platform's fallback).

## Open questions

- [x] Are three tiers enough? → **yes: read, operate, administer** (industry standard: separation of read, operate and admin, under the user's standing instruction of 2026-09-30)
- [x] Which actions need a second administrator? → **granting the top tier, erasing a business or a person, mass suspension, rotating the signing key, republishing currency; not a single suspension** (industry standard: dual control on the irreversible and the wide, under the user's standing instruction of 2026-09-30)
- [x] What if there is only one administrator? → **the guarded actions are refused until there are two; break-glass is the audited exception** (industry standard, under the user's standing instruction of 2026-09-30)
- [x] How is "at once" met without a cache timeout? → **an event, consumed by every gateway replica; the poll stays only as a backstop** (industry standard, under the user's standing instruction of 2026-09-30)
- [x] Who aggregates the audit? → **reporting-svc, by merge at read; no copy** (industry standard: the read-side service, no second store of sensitive rows, under the user's standing instruction of 2026-09-30)
- [x] Does a downgrade credit go back as cash? → **no: a credit note held as account credit; cash only by a payment reversal** (industry standard, under the user's standing instruction of 2026-09-30)
- [x] Does a customer's erasure reach the business's records when the platform erases a shopper login? → **no: the login is the platform's, the customer record is the business's; the link is cut and the business is told** (industry standard: controller and processor roles, under the user's standing instruction of 2026-09-30)
- [x] Does the owner get the export before a business is erased on demand? → **yes, unless a legal reference says otherwise** (industry standard: data portability first, under the user's standing instruction of 2026-09-30)

## Acceptance

## Screens

- **Platform console → Staff:** the platform's people with their tier and second-factor state; **Add** (operator or support) and **Change tier** (administrators only); a banner when there is one administrator.
- **Platform console → Approvals:** the requests waiting (erase a business, erase a person, republish currency, rotate the key, grant a tier, suspend many), **Approve** / **Refuse** (disabled with a plain reason on one's own request).
