## Problem

The platform can already say who may do a thing (permissions and tiers). It cannot say **how much** a person may do alone, or that some things need a **second person**. Two controls exist, each on its own: purchase orders wait for someone whose ceiling covers them, and a till discount or return over the cashier's limit is refused. Everything else that moves money or stock is limited only by a role check: a storekeeper writes off any quantity, any manager sets a price to nearly nothing, a cashier issues a gift card of any amount, one person changes a supplier's bank account and the next payment run pays it, one manager posts a manual journal and files the VAT return, one platform administrator rotates the signing key for every business.

Each of these was found as its own gap and each was parked with the same note: it needs a product decision on thresholds and who approves. The risk of answering them one by one is nine different mechanisms with nine different refusal shapes and no single place where an owner can see or change them.

## Outcome

- **One question, asked the same way everywhere.** Before an action that has a value (money, a quantity, a percentage) the owning service asks "may this caller do this much alone?" and gets one of three answers: yes, no (with the ceiling and what was asked), or "keep it for a second person".
- **The business sets the ceilings.** An owner opens one settings screen, sees every controlled action with its owning area, and sets a ceiling per tier and, where it makes sense, a second-person rule. Nothing is set until they set it (with the few exceptions named below).
- **A refusal is one stable shape.** `403 AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED` names the action, the caller's ceiling, what was asked, the unit and currency, and who could do it.
- **A manager can cover a cashier at the till** by entering their own PIN or password on the cashier's screen; the action goes through under the cashier's session and both are named on the record.
- **Where a second person is needed, the request is kept, not lost.** The maker gets `202` with a request they can watch; approvers are told in the app and by email; one of them, never the maker, approves or rejects; the action is done once, in the same transaction as the approval, and the record names both people.
- **Existing controls keep working exactly as they do.** Purchase-order approval and the return policy appear in the same catalogue and the same inbox, without a change of code, status or data.

## Who and where

- **Personas** ([PRD §2](../PRD.md)):
  - The **owner**, who sets the rules and is the approver of last resort.
  - The **store manager** and **area manager** (a custom role on the MANAGER tier), who cover cashiers and approve requests.
  - The **finance / purchasing user**, who makes and approves procurement requests.
  - The **cashier** and **storekeeper**, who are the makers most often refused or held.
  - The **platform administrator**, for platform-wide actions.
- **Channels:** POS (step-up), back-office (inbox, rules screen), platform console (platform-wide actions). Online storefront: none (a shopper never holds authority).
- **Scope:** rules are per business. A request belongs to a store or to the whole business; who may decide follows the store, as everything else store-held does.
- **Roles that can write:**
  - Rules: OWNER only. A control that a manager could loosen is not a control. Every change is on the trail and announced to the business's other owners.
  - A request: whoever may take the underlying action.
  - A decision: a holder of the action's approver permission (named in the catalogue, from `Permissions`), whose own ceiling covers the value, with access to the store, and who is not the maker.
- **Sandbox tenant:** behaves the same, rules and requests included. Notifications go in-app only, as everywhere in a sandbox (`TenantProfiles.Profile.sandbox()`).

## Scope

- **In:**
  1. **The rules and the shared decision** (tenant-svc, common-service). An action catalogue, a rule per business per action, a cached reader, a pure decision, one refusal shape.
  2. **The pending-approval building block** (common-service, opted into by each service): the table, the API, once-only execution, expiry, events, notifications, the Approvals inbox.
  3. **Procurement money first** (purchase-svc; the VAT return's filing and correction are pricing-svc's): bank-detail change and the first payment to new details, payment runs, manual journals, period re-open, payee override, VAT return, landed charges, invoice variance, scorecard corrections, special orders, the purchase-order adapter.
  4. **Manager step-up** (iam-svc): the till override (`/auth/step-up`). Platform-wide actions are slice 10.
  5. **Till and refund money** (order-svc, payment-svc, customer-svc): tiered refunds, voids, discount step-up, price override at the till, gift cards, store credit and points, large cash, cancelling an order being picked (the forced cancel), a till close with a large variance, a payment held for review, borrowing a colleague's till session (iam-svc).
  6. **Stock** (inventory-svc, purchase-svc's vendor returns): write-offs and adjustments, transfers, unordered receipts, bond release, yield loss, recall close, wave short-close, cycle-count post (the vendor return past on-hand is purchase-svc's).
  7. **Prices and range** (pricing-svc, product-svc): deep markdown (the ladder's ceiling and pricing's own finding, one key), price set, promotion, discontinue, range review close.
  8. **People and messages** (tenant-svc, notification-svc): granting the OWNER tier, time corrections that raise paid hours, message-template go-live.
  9. **One view of everything** and the larger chains: the legacy controls shown in the rules screen, several approvers above a higher band, escalation, delegation while on leave.
  10. **Platform-scope approvals** (iam-svc, tenant-svc, common-service, platform console): approvals that belong to no business, for the `platform.*` keys. See the section below. It needs only the block of slice 2 plus the platform tiers of [platform-administration](platform-administration.md) slice 2, so it is built as soon as both exist.
- **Out, on purpose:**
  - **Rewriting purchase-order approval and the return policy.** They keep their state, data, codes and screens; see Decisions. They are listed in the catalogue so an owner sees one picture.
  - **Offline step-up.** A till that is offline cannot ask iam-svc, so it offers no step-up; an over-ceiling action is refused as it is today. The alternative, honouring a manager's card read offline, cannot be told from a stolen card.
  - **Approving your own request through a second account you also hold.** The platform compares people (`userId`), not sessions; two accounts held by one human is an identity-management matter, not an approvals one.
  - **Automatic approval below a ceiling as a separate mode** (for example auto-applying a small repricing proposal). A rule below the ceiling already means "no one else needed"; a person's click on a repricing proposal stays a feature of pricing-svc's page.
  - **Manager overrides that are new features rather than approvals** (re-sequencing a wave, hand-adjusting a depot proposal, changing a cross-dock split, forcing a route for a split order). Their pages are written ([replenishment-overrides](replenishment-overrides.md), [fulfilment-overrides](fulfilment-overrides.md)) and none of them takes an approval key: a depot line, a cross-dock share and a wave sequence are changed by a `stock.transfer` holder with a required reason and a recorded, reversible override; a re-route is management at both stores with a reason. The two that do need an authority are the forced cancel (`sales.cancel-in-pick`) and a wave closed short (`stock.wave-short-close`), both in the catalogue.
  - **"Too many by one person in a period."** That is [exception-alerts](exception-alerts.md); this page only makes each override a countable fact (`ApprovalDecided`, `approved_by` on the record).
  - **Approving on behalf of a business as the platform administrator.** A platform administrator is never an approver for a business's requests; only for `platform.*` ones.
  - **Removing an age-restriction override.** The catalogue's finding there is a justification on a change that is already refused when it loosens; it belongs to product-svc's page.

## The two shapes, and which action takes which

**(a) Tiered authority.** The action has a value. Each tier (and each custom role, if named) has a ceiling for it. Within the caller's ceiling the caller does it alone. Above it, the caller is refused `403 AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED`, and either someone whose ceiling covers it does it in their own session, or (at a till) that person steps up on the caller's screen and the action goes through under the caller's session, the approver named. Nothing is kept: the person is standing there.

**(b) Second person (maker-checker).** The action is kept as a request and a different person with the authority approves or rejects; the action happens on approval. Used where nobody is waiting at a counter and where the maker is the person the control protects against.

One rule can carry both: ceilings say who may do it alone, and `overCeiling` says what happens to the rest, `REFUSE` (shape a) or `PENDING` (shape b). A rule may also say `secondPersonAlways` (shape b for every value, including the owner's own). The catalogue fixes which modes each action allows, by these industry practices:

- **A, never kept:** a customer or a queue is waiting (voids, discounts, price override, large cash tender, refunds at the till, cancelling an order being picked, wave short-close, forcing a session closed, a vendor return past on-hand: an override of a refusal, not a decision to wait for).
- **B, always:** value that leaves the business or a change no one should make alone, however small (a supplier's bank details, filing a VAT return, granting the OWNER tier, template go-live for legally sensitive messages, platform-wide irreversible actions). Standard maker-checker.
- **A or B, the business's choice:** a value with ceilings and a back office that can wait (write-offs, transfers, receipts without an order, landed charges, journals, deep markdowns and prices, gift-card issue). The default mode in the catalogue is what a new rule starts with.

## Where settings live, and how services read them

- **One owner: tenant-svc, `authority_rules`.** It already owns each business's other settings that every service reads (`Plans`, the fx-rate table, `TenantProfiles`), its staff and its custom roles, so a rule that speaks about tiers and roles sits beside them.
- **Against "plan limits are enforced by the service that owns the thing counted":** that rule is about **counting**, where the owner has the numbers. A ceiling on a value is a business's policy setting, not a count; it is set once and read by many. What still holds is the enforcement: **the service that owns the action always makes the decision** (inventory-svc refuses a write-off, order-svc a discount, purchase-svc a payment run), from its own rows and figures, and never asks tenant-svc to decide for it. tenant-svc only stores the rule and answers it.
- **The reader** is `Authority` in common-service, shaped like `FxRates`: one cached `GET /admin/tenant/authority-rules?keys=…` per business, a minute's cache, stale-if-error up to the platform's configured window. It hands the owning service a `Rule`; the pure `AuthorityDecision.decide(rule, callerTiers, measured)` (like `SpendAuthority`, no database, no clock) gives the answer. `Fx` translates a value in a foreign currency to home currency at the business's own rate; **no rate means the caller is treated as over the ceiling** (fails closed, as spend authority does), except a caller with unlimited authority.
- **When the rule cannot be read** (tenant-svc down and nothing cached): actions marked `OPEN` in the catalogue proceed as if no rule were set (a sale never stops for a settings blip: till actions, refunds, voids); actions marked `CLOSED` are refused `503 APPROVAL_SETTINGS_UNAVAILABLE` (money-out and irreversible back-office actions: payment run, bank details, journals, VAT filing, platform actions).
- **No rule row means off:** no ceiling, no second person. Once a rule exists, a tier it does not name has **no authority** for that action (the same as discounts today: a role absent from the map may not discount), except OWNER, which is unlimited unless the rule caps it explicitly.
- **A key a service does not enforce yet cannot be set.** `Approvals.CATALOGUE` marks each key with the release that enforces it, and tenant-svc refuses to save a rule for a key not yet enforced (`422 APPROVAL_ACTION_NOT_ENFORCED`), so the screen never promises a control that does not bite (the way `Plans.CATALOGUE` works).
- **Legacy sources stay** (see Decisions): `purchasing.order` reads `storeql.purchase.approval.limits` and `sales.discount` reads `storeql.order.discount.max-percent` until a business has a rule of its own for that key; `sales.return` reads `return_policies`.

### Where the safe default is ON, and why

Everything else is off until the business sets it. Three things are not a business's choice:

1. **Nobody approves their own request.** An invariant, not a setting: the maker's `userId` is refused as approver in the service and proved in a test on every adopting action. It costs nothing when no second-person rule is on.
2. **A supplier's bank-details change is confirmed by a second person before the first payment to the new details**, whenever the business has at least two holders of `finance.payments`. Business-email compromise (a fraudster asks to change a bank account) is the commonest way small and mid-size businesses lose money to a supplier, and the platform today only warns (`BANK_DETAILS_CHANGED_RECENTLY`). It carries no figure: no amount or duration is assumed. A one-person business cannot satisfy it, so it keeps the warning. An owner can switch it off with a reason on the trail, never a manager.
3. **Platform-wide actions by a platform administrator** (signing-key rotation, republishing every tenant's currency, mass suspension, erasure, granting the administrator tier) require a written reason and a fresh second-factor answer always, and a second platform administrator's approval whenever the platform has two or more (the irreversible ones are refused outright while there is only one; see "Platform-scope approvals"). The POS sweep needs the reason and the fresh factor and the OPERATOR tier, no second person. One call by one person changes every business at once; no business can set this for the platform.

## The pending-approval mechanism (shape b)

- **One block, adopted per service.** common-service holds `PendingApprovals` (repository, service, resource base, `ApprovalAction` interface). A service opts in through `ServiceSettings`, which adds a repeatable migration (`R__approvals.sql`, `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS`, from a shared Flyway location) that creates the tables **in that service's own schema**: database-per-service holds, no shared table. The common `afterMigrate` uuid-v7 checks apply to them as to every uuid column, and the backup-drill covers them (the CHECKs use no function that a restore's empty search path could miss).
- **`approval_requests`** (per service): `id`, `tenant_id` (null only for `scope = PLATFORM`, slice 10, which also adds `scope` and `target_tenant_id`), `store_id` (null for business-wide), `action_key`, `subject_type` and `subject_id` (the thing acted on), `maker_id`, `maker_tier` (a snapshot), `value_amount` and `value_currency` and `value_home_amount` (money), or `value_quantity`, or `value_percent`, `payload` (the frozen request as JSON, with a schema version), `subject_version` (a hash or version of the state the maker saw), `idempotency_key` (unique per business), `status` (PENDING, APPROVED, REJECTED, EXPIRED, CANCELLED), `approvals_needed`, `expires_at`, `requested_at`, `decided_at`, `executed_at`. A partial unique index allows one PENDING request per (business, action, subject) unless the action's catalogue entry says many.
- **`approval_decisions`** (append-only): `id`, `tenant_id`, `request_id`, `approver_id`, `approver_tier`, `decision` (APPROVE or REJECT), `note`, `decided_at`. One row per person, never updated.
- **Making a request.** The resource calls `approvals.gate(ctx, actionKey, measured, payload, subjectRef)`. The gate returns PROCEED (no rule, or within the caller's ceiling), REFUSE (shape a) or PENDING. On PENDING it writes the request and its outbox event on the same transaction and the resource answers `202` with the request and a `Location`. The same call with the same `Idempotency-Key` answers the same request. The maker may cancel their own request while PENDING.
- **Approving.** `POST /admin/approvals/{id}/approve` (`Idempotency-Key`), on one transaction: lock the request row `FOR UPDATE`; refuse unless PENDING and not past `expires_at`; refuse the maker; check the approver holds the permission, the ceiling covers the value, and the store; append the decision; when the number of distinct approvals reaches `approvals_needed`, call the action's `ApprovalAction.execute(request, approver, tx)` **on that same transaction**, set APPROVED and `executed_at`, and write `ApprovalDecided` to the outbox. Executors are written like the existing `…Tx` methods (they take the transaction; they publish their own events through the outbox). So the action happens **once**, and the approval and the action commit or roll back together.
- **The state may have moved.** The executor re-reads the subject and compares `subject_version`; a change is `409 APPROVAL_SUBJECT_CHANGED` and nothing is done. If the executor refuses (stock gone, period closed), the whole transaction rolls back and the approver sees the refusal; the request stays PENDING for a reject or a later retry, and expires as the rest do.
- **Rejecting** (`/reject`, a note required) ends it REJECTED and tells the maker.
- **Expiry** is a business setting per action (`expiryHours` on the rule; off means it stays until decided or cancelled). A scheduled sweep in each adopting service marks EXPIRED (never executes) and publishes `ApprovalExpired`.
- **Listing.** Each adopting service serves `GET /admin/approvals` (cursor, filter by status, action, store, mine/waiting for me) and `GET /admin/approvals/count` for a badge. The decision was between one aggregated view and one per service: **per service**, because the owning service holds the payload and runs the action, and a central view would either copy payloads or call every service on every read. The app's inbox fans out over the owning services the catalogue names and merges by time, saying so when one does not answer.
- **Services with their own state machine** (purchase-svc's `PENDING_APPROVAL` orders, and a flagged supplier invoice) implement `ApprovalSource` instead of using the table: the same list, count and events, with approve and reject delegating to the code that exists.
- **Notifying approvers.** `ApprovalRequested` carries the action, store, value, approver permission and expiry. notification-svc asks tenant-svc who holds that permission at that store (`GET /admin/tenant/approvers?permission=&storeId=&excluding=`, the maker excluded, tiers checked against the rule's ceilings) and sends an in-app notification and an email in the platform's own words (never a catalogue message a business can reword); the maker is told of the decision and of expiry. When nobody's ceiling covers a value the request goes to the OWNERs (the escalation the purchase-order gap asked for).
- **Audit.** Every request, decision and execution is a row on the audit trail with the maker, approver, action, value and request id; and the affected record itself carries `approved_by` and `approval_id`, added by the adopting service's own migration where the record is append-only (a movement, a refund, a journal). The trail keeps rows for a maker or approver who has since left.
- **Maker leaves or loses the role.** The request stays valid: it is the business's request, not the person's. The approver sees "the maker no longer holds this authority / is deactivated" beside it and may still approve or reject; nothing is executed in the maker's name later. An approver who loses the role loses the right to decide from that moment; decisions already made stand.
- **No second approver exists.** Saving a second-person rule is refused `409 APPROVAL_NEEDS_SECOND_APPROVER` unless at least two people hold the approver permission at the scope. If staff leave afterwards, existing requests stay PENDING, the OWNERs are told, and a request is never approved by its maker.
- **Tenant isolation.** `tenant_id` from the JWT is the first condition on every query and every row. A request id of another business is `404`. A store-held approver sees and decides only requests at their stores; a business-wide request (no store) needs an approver held to no store, as staff and reports do.

## Platform-scope approvals (slice 10)

The `platform.*` actions change every business at once or erase one. There is no business whose rules, approvers or inbox apply, so they are a second scope of the same mechanism, not a second mechanism.

- **Same block, one more column.** `approval_requests` (in iam-svc and tenant-svc, the two services that own a `platform.*` action) gains `scope` (`BUSINESS`, the default, or `PLATFORM`) and `target_tenant_id` (the business a platform action names, nullable). A CHECK says `(scope = 'PLATFORM') = (tenant_id IS NULL)`: a platform request carries no business of its own, and the every-query rule "`tenant_id` first" becomes "`scope` and, for a business request, `tenant_id`". The uuid-v7 checks apply as everywhere. `approval_decisions` gains the same two columns.
- **Rules are not per business.** No `authority_rules` row exists for a `platform.*` key and none can be saved (`422 APPROVAL_MODE_NOT_ALLOWED`); the catalogue fixes the mode (B always, except as stated per key) because no business may loosen a control over the whole platform. Expiry hours, escalation and the approver permission come from platform configuration.
- **Who may decide:** a login with the `ADMINISTRATOR` platform tier ([platform-administration](platform-administration.md) slice 2), other than the requester, after a fresh second-factor answer. An `OPERATOR`, `SUPPORT` and every business role, the OWNER included, are refused (`403 PLATFORM_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` or `APPROVAL_ABOVE_AUTHORITY`); the decision of a platform administrator on a *business* request is refused (`403`, unchanged from the Out list above).
- **Who is asked:** notification-svc reads the administrators from iam-svc (`GET /platform/staff?tier=ADMINISTRATOR`, service to service, the requester excluded) and sends the same in-app and email notice. The platform console has its own inbox served at `/platform/approvals` by iam-svc and tenant-svc and merged by the console, as the app merges services for a business.
- **One administrator.** The keys split by what cannot be undone. `platform.administrator-grant`, `platform.tenant-erase` and `platform.shopper-erase` are irreversible and never urgent: with fewer than two active administrators they are refused `409 PLATFORM_SECOND_ADMINISTRATOR_REQUIRED` and the only unilateral path is the audited break-glass of [sign-in-protection](sign-in-protection.md). `platform.signing-key-rotate`, `platform.currency-republish` and `platform.tenant-mass-suspend` can be the remedy for the very incident that leaves one administrator, so with one administrator they proceed on a written reason and a fresh second factor (the step-up call of the next section, `actionKey` naming the platform action), are announced to the security contact, and are on the platform trail. This settles a contradiction between this page (one administrator: step-up) and platform-administration (one administrator: refuse) by industry practice: dual control where the harm is irreversible, accountability where speed may matter.
- **Codes and vocabulary.** The platform page's `PLATFORM_APPROVAL_PENDING` is this page's `202` with the request; `PLATFORM_APPROVAL_SAME_ADMINISTRATOR` is `403 APPROVAL_SELF_DECISION`; `PLATFORM_SECOND_ADMINISTRATOR_REQUIRED` and `PLATFORM_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` stay (they are about tiers, not requests).
- **Audit.** Every platform request, decision and execution is also a `platform_audit` row (tenant-svc), written by the executing service in the same transaction as the action ([platform-administration](platform-administration.md) slice 2), never by a generic filter.
- **Tests.** `PendingApprovalsIT.aPlatformAdminIsNotABusinessApprover`, `PlatformApprovalIT.*` as in Acceptance; plus `PlatformScopeIT.aPlatformRequestHasNoBusiness` (the CHECK), `.onlyAdministratorsDecide`, `.oneAdministratorSplit` (irreversible keys refused, reversible ones proceed on a reason and a fresh factor).

## Manager step-up at the till (iam-svc)

- `POST /auth/step-up` (a signed-in staff member): `{approverId, secret, actionKey, storeId}`. The secret is the approver's password, and a second-factor code if they have enrolled one, or a **till PIN** the approver sets themselves (`PUT /auth/step-up/pin`, only a hash kept; the PIN works only at a step-up, never to sign in). Answer: a single-use token bound to the business, store, action, and caller. A wrong secret counts as a failed sign-in at the gateway's brute-force filter, as a wrong second factor does.
- The owning service calls `POST /auth/step-up/consume {token, actionKey, storeId}` (breaker, timeout; unreachable means refused) and gets the approver's tiers, permissions and stores. It then asks `AuthorityDecision` **for the approver**. Covered: the action proceeds under the caller's session with `approved_by` set. Not covered: `403 AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED` naming the approver's own ceiling.
- `409 STEPUP_SELF` if approver is caller; `403 STEPUP_INVALID` for a used, expired, or mismatched token. Tokens are stored hashed, expire in a platform-configured short life, and are consumed by one action.
- A platform-wide action uses the same call with the platform administrator's fresh second factor and reason (slice 10).

## Data and flow

- **Owned by tenant-svc:**
  - `authority_rules`: `id`, `tenant_id`, `action_key`, `over_ceiling` (REFUSE or PENDING), `second_person_always` (boolean), `expiry_hours` (null = never), `approver_permission` (default from the catalogue), `enabled`, `version`, `changed_by`, `changed_at`. Unique on (`tenant_id`, `action_key`).
  - `authority_rule_tiers`: `rule_id`, `tenant_id`, `tier_or_role` (a tier, or a custom role's name), `max_value` (NUMERIC(18,4), home currency), `max_quantity`, `max_percent`, `unlimited`. A tier missing here has no authority.
  - `authority_rule_bands` (slice 9): `rule_id`, `above_value`, `approvals_needed`. More than one person above a higher band.
  - `authority_rule_history` (append-only): every save, the full previous and new rule, who and when.
- **Owned by each adopting service:** `approval_requests` and `approval_decisions` as above; `approved_by` and `approval_id` on the records its actions write.
- **Needs from other services:** rules from tenant-svc (REST, cached); approvers from tenant-svc (REST, by notification-svc); the fx rate through `FxRates`; step-up from iam-svc (REST). Never a join.
- **Events published** (outbox, idempotent consumers):
  - `AuthorityRuleChanged` (tenant-svc, `storeql.tenant.authority-rule-changed`: tenant, action, version, who): notification-svc tells the other owners; readers evict their cache on it (the cache's minute stays as a backstop).
  - `ApprovalRequested` (`storeql.approvals.requested`): notification-svc notifies approvers.
  - `ApprovalDecided` (`storeql.approvals.decided`, with APPROVED or REJECTED, approver, `executed`): notification-svc tells the maker; reporting-svc counts overrides per person and store for the exceptions report.
  - `ApprovalExpired` (`storeql.approvals.expired`): notification-svc tells the maker and approvers.
- **Retryable writes (Idempotency-Key):** every gated action that already has one keeps it and gains it where the catalogue action had none; `PUT` of a rule; approve, reject, cancel; step-up.
- **Endpoints.**
  - tenant-svc: `GET /admin/tenant/authority-rules` (management reads, services read with the caller's tenant), `GET …/catalogue` (keys, owning service, measure, permission, allowed modes, `OPEN`/`CLOSED`, whether enforced, default), `PUT /admin/tenant/authority-rules/{actionKey}` and `DELETE` (OWNER), `GET …/{actionKey}/history`, `GET /admin/tenant/approvers`.
  - Each adopting service: `GET /admin/approvals`, `GET /admin/approvals/count`, `GET /admin/approvals/{id}`, `POST …/{id}/approve`, `…/reject`, `…/cancel`. Via the gateway under `/api/v1/{service}/admin/approvals`.
  - iam-svc: `PUT /auth/step-up/pin`, `POST /auth/step-up`, `POST /auth/step-up/consume`.
- **New error codes:**
  - `403 AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED`, with `details` = `{actionKey, ceiling, asked, unit, currency, callerTier, approverPermission, reason}`, where `reason` is `OVER_CEILING`, `NO_RATE`, `NO_AUTHORITY` (a tier the rule does not name).
  - `202` (not an error): the request kept, with its id.
  - `403 APPROVAL_SELF_DECISION` · `403 APPROVAL_ABOVE_AUTHORITY` (approver's ceiling does not cover) · `409 APPROVAL_NOT_PENDING` · `409 APPROVAL_EXPIRED` · `409 APPROVAL_SUBJECT_CHANGED` · `409 APPROVAL_ALREADY_PENDING` · `404 APPROVAL_NOT_FOUND`.

  - `409 STEPUP_SELF` · `403 STEPUP_INVALID`.
  - Legacy codes (`ORDER_DISCOUNT_EXCEEDS_AUTHORITY`, `ORDER_RETURN_NEEDS_MANAGER`, `PURCHASE_APPROVAL_*`) are unchanged and also carry the same `details`.

## Money, time and limits

- **Currency:** a ceiling is in the business's home currency. A value in another currency is translated through `Fx` at the business's own rate; no rate fails closed (`reason: NO_RATE`). Legacy purchasing ceilings stay per currency as they are.
- **Ledger postings:** none of its own. An approved action posts as it always did, on the approver's transaction.
- **Dates:** `requested_at`, `decided_at`, `executed_at`, `expires_at` in UTC. Expiry hours are a setting on the rule; no default.
- **Plan limits:** none. (The approval rules are not an entitlement; every plan has them.)

## Constraints

- **Golden rules:** 1 (each service its own tables; rules read over REST, never a join), 3 (tenant from the JWT, first condition; approver identity from the JWT or the consumed step-up, never a body), 6 and 7 (events through the outbox; consumers idempotent), 8 (decisions, rule history, movements and refunds append-only), 9 (resources stay thin: the gate and the executor are `service/`), 10 (payloads are DTOs, never entities), 11 (Idempotency-Key), 13 (BigDecimal, currency named), 14 (UTC), 15 (validate every input), UUIDv7 everywhere (`Ids`).
- **Exactly once.** The row lock plus the status check on the same transaction as the action is the guarantee; the idempotency key covers the client retry.
- **Existing businesses.** With no rule row nothing changes, except the three default-on items, which apply only to a business with two or more eligible people (bank details) or to the platform's own administrators. Businesses using `PURCHASE_APPROVAL_LIMITS` or the discount ceilings see no difference.
- **Flow guards:** `flow-guard-comprehensive` and `flow-guard-runtime` stay green; the catalogue's adoption is behind rules that are off, so they see no change.
- **Location-neutral:** no amount, currency, period, or country is assumed; the ceilings are the business's; times are UTC and shown in the store's zone; a tax return's second person applies to whatever return the business files.
- **Performance:** the decision is a cached read and a pure function; nothing on the sale's path calls tenant-svc synchronously except on a cold cache.

## Screens

- **Admin shell, Settings, "Approvals and limits" (OWNER).** One row per catalogue action grouped by area: what it controls, its owning area, and a rule editor: a ceiling per tier and named custom role (money in the home currency, a quantity or a percent, as the action measures), over-ceiling mode (Refuse, or Keep for approval where allowed), "always ask a second person", expiry hours, approver permission. The page refuses a key not yet enforced (greyed with the reason). The legacy controls (purchase-order limits, discount ceilings, the return policy) appear read-only with a link to where they are set; a business that has saved its own rule sees which source wins. A history drawer per action.
- **Admin shell, Approvals inbox** (a badge in the shell). Tabs: Waiting for me, Mine, Decided. Each row: the action in words (`status_labels.dart`, never the key), what and where, value via `AppFormat.money`, the maker by name, age and expiry. The detail shows the frozen request and, for an approver, Approve and Reject (a note required to reject). Words for every refusal. Merged from each service; a missing service is named.
- **POS.** When the till gets `403 AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED` on a discount, price override, void, large cash tender, refund or take-over, a "Manager approval" dialog asks who (names of eligible people at the store) and their PIN or password (and code); it retries the same action with the token. The cashier's screen names the approver on the receipt line after. Offline: the dialog is not offered.
- **Platform console.** Platform-wide actions ask for a reason and a second-factor code; with two administrators the action shows as waiting in their inbox.
- **Storefront:** none.

## Decisions

- **Purchase-order approval and the return policy are kept exactly as they are, and listed** (2026-09-30, industry standard, under the user's standing instruction). `purchasing.order` keeps `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `purchase_order_approvals`, `storeql.purchase.approval.limits` (`PURCHASE_APPROVAL_LIMITS`) and `SpendAuthority`; its unresolved gaps (escalation when nobody's ceiling covers, several approvers above a higher band) are added later through the rule's bands and the escalation to OWNERs, by `ApprovalSource`, not by moving its data. `sales.return` keeps `return_policies` and `ORDER_RETURN_NEEDS_MANAGER`. Both appear in the catalogue, the rules screen (read-only) and the inbox (through `ApprovalSource` for purchase orders). Why: they are built, tested and running in businesses; a migration would risk breaking `PURCHASE_APPROVAL_LIMITS` and the policy for no gain. When a business saves a rule of its own for `purchasing.order` or `sales.discount`, the rule wins for that business; the configuration remains the answer for everyone else. Configuration is per deployment, a rule is per business, which is the gap the rules close.
- **The manager PIN step-up excluded by [return-controls](return-controls.md) ("Out, on purpose") is built here, for every till action, not for returns alone.** That page's text stands for its own build; this page replaces the exclusion for the platform because the industry practice is a manager's credential on the cashier's own till, and because otherwise each action would invent its own way to cover a cashier. Returns keep their own-session rule until the step-up is adopted in slice 5, when a manager may either complete it in their own session or step up.
- **Ceilings measure a value, and values are in the home currency.** Not a count, so this is not a plan limit; the owning service always decides.
- **A rule for a key not yet enforced is refused.** The catalogue is the list and a key outside it is refused rather than promised, following `Plans.CATALOGUE`.
- **No numbers are shipped as defaults** (no amount, percentage, duration or count), apart from those already in force (`storeql.order.discount.max-percent`'s defaults stay). The three default-on cases carry no figure.
- **One naming scheme for action keys, reconciled across every page (2026-09-30).** A key is `<area>.<action>` in lower-case kebab: the area is one of `stock`, `purchasing`, `finance`, `sales`, `till`, `customer`, `pricing`, `catalog`, `staff`, `notify`, `platform`; the owner is the service that enforces it (the catalogue table above is the single list; a key not in it does not exist, and `Approvals.CATALOGUE` in common-service is generated from it). - **Findings that are facts, not decisions, are not approvals (2026-09-30).** A transfer that arrives short and a receipt that books a loss record something that already happened on the dock; approving it would only make it disappear. [transfer-discrepancies](transfer-discrepancies.md) originally named `stock.transfer` for "a receipt with a big loss"; that is now the alert metric `transfers.discrepancy_value` on [exception-alerts](exception-alerts.md), and `stock.transfer` covers only releasing a transfer.
- **Decision, not policy, for who approves:** the approver must hold the action's permission and a ceiling that covers the value; a tier a rule does not name has none. This is the same principle the discount ceilings already use.
